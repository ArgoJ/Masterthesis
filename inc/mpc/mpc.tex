\section{Model Predictive Control and Stability}
\label{sec:mpc-stability}

This section summarizes the model predictive control (MPC) framework as well as
the conditions that are required to ensure the stability of the control loop in 
optimal control configurations with a finite time horizon.


\subsection{Problem Formulation}
\label{subsec:mpc-problem}

For the nominal MPC setup considered in this work, the following finite-horizon optimal control problem  
is solved at each time step $k$ given the current system state $x_k$~\citeSec{2.2,3.3}{rawlingsModelPredictiveControl2020,gruneNonlinearModelPredictive2017}:
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:mpc-problem}
    \begin{aligned}
        \min_{(x_{i|k})_{i=0}^N, (u_{i|k})_{i=0}^{N-1}} \; 
                        & & J \left(x_k, (u_{i|k})_{i=0}^{N-1} \right) &= \sum_{i=0}^{N-1} \ell(x_{i|k}, u_{i|k}) + V_f(x_{N|k}) \\
        \text{s.t.} \;  & & x_{i+1|k}   &= f(x_{i|k}, u_{i|k}),     && i = 0, \dots, N-1, \\
                        & & x_{i|k}     &\in \mathcal{X},           && i = 0, \dots, N-1, \\
                        & & u_{i|k}     &\in \mathcal{U},           && i = 0, \dots, N-1, \\
                        & & x_{N|k}     &\in \mathcal{X}_f, \\
                        & & x_{0|k}     &= x_k.
    \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Here, $N \in \mathbb{N}$ denotes the prediction horizon, $\ell: \mathcal{X} \times \mathcal{U} \to \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}$ is the stage cost function, 
and $V_f: \mathcal{X}_f \to \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}$ represents the terminal cost function, which is defined over the terminal constraint set 
$\mathcal{X}_f \subseteq \mathcal{X}$. To distinguish optimization from closed-loop variables, 
$x_{i|k}$ and $u_{i|k}$ denote the predicted state and input at horizon step $i$, initialized at sampling time $k$. 
The sequences $(x_{i|k})_{i=0}^N$ and $(u_{i|k})_{i=0}^{N-1}$ serve as optimization variables constrained by the 
sets $\mathcal{X}$ and $\mathcal{U}$~\citeSec{3.2}{gruneNonlinearModelPredictive2017}. Furthermore, the optimal value function is defined as 
$V_N(x_k) := J \left( x_k, (u_{i\vert{}k}^\star)_{i=0}^{N-1} \right)$.

For this work, the stage cost is chosen as the quadratic cost function with positive definite weighting matrices $Q \succ 0$ and $R \succ 0$, 
which is defined as follows~\citeSec{1.3}{rawlingsModelPredictiveControl2020}:
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:stage-cost}
    \ell(x_{i|k}, u_{i|k}) = x_{i|k}^\top Q x_{i|k} + u_{i|k}^\top R u_{i|k}.
\end{equation}
Following the receding horizon principle, only the first optimal control input $u_{0|k}^\star$ of the sequence is applied to the system.
This implicitly defines the closed-loop MPC feedback policy $\pi_0: \mathcal{X}_N \to \mathcal{U}$ as
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:mpc-feedback-policy}
    \pi_0(x_k) := u_{0|k}^\star,
\end{equation}
yielding the nominal closed-loop dynamics $x_{k+1} = f(x_k, \pi_0(x_k))$. 
The optimal control problem \labelcref{eq:mpc-problem} is then repeated at the subsequent step with 
the updated state~\citeSec{2.2,3.1}{rawlingsModelPredictiveControl2020,gruneNonlinearModelPredictive2017}.


\subsection{Terminal Ingredients and their Construction}
\label{subsec:terminal-set-cost} 

To establish recursive feasibility and closed-loop stability of nominal MPC, the optimal control problem \labelcref{eq:mpc-problem} 
incorporates three central design ingredients: 
the terminal constraint set $\mathcal{X}_f \subseteq \mathcal{X}$, an auxiliary local control law $\pi_f: \mathcal{X}_f \to \mathcal{U}$, 
and the terminal cost function $V_f: \mathcal{X}_f \to \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}$~
\citeAsm{2.14,5.9}{rawlingsModelPredictiveControl2020,gruneNonlinearModelPredictive2017}.

Specifically, these terminal ingredients must satisfy state and input constraints, positive invariance of the terminal set, 
and local Lyapunov decrease of the terminal cost:
\begin{subequations}
    \label{eq:terminal-conditions}
    \begin{align}
        \mathcal{X}_f &\subseteq \mathcal{X}, \label{eq:term-state-constraint} \\
        \pi_f(x) &\in \mathcal{U}, \qquad &\forall x \in \mathcal{X}_f, \label{eq:term-input-constraint} \\
        f(x, \pi_f(x)) &\in \mathcal{X}_f, \qquad &\forall x \in \mathcal{X}_f, \label{eq:term-positive-invariance} \\
        V_f(f(x, \pi_f(x))) - V_f(x) &\leq -\ell(x, \pi_f(x)), \qquad &\forall x \in \mathcal{X}_f. \label{eq:term-cost-decrease}
    \end{align}
\end{subequations}

The conditions \labelcref{eq:term-state-constraint} and \labelcref{eq:term-input-constraint} ensure that terminal states and their corresponding 
local control input remain within the admissible state and input bounds $\mathcal{X}$ and $\mathcal{U}$. 
Additionally, positive invariance in \labelcref{eq:term-positive-invariance} ensures that every state in 
$\mathcal{X}_f$ remains in $\mathcal{X}_f$ under the local controller $\pi_f(x)$~\citeDef{2.9}{rawlingsModelPredictiveControl2020}. 
This property is essential for proving recursive feasibility, as it enables the terminal state of a feasible solution at horizon $N$ 
to be extended by the local control input $\pi_f(x_{N|k}^\star)$. This yields a feasible candidate trajectory for the subsequent 
sampling step $k+1$, as further described in \cref{subsec:closed-loop-stability}~\citeSec{2.4.2}{rawlingsModelPredictiveControl2020}.
The terminal cost decrease condition in \labelcref{eq:term-cost-decrease} acts as a local Lyapunov condition within $\mathcal{X}_f$. 
While positive invariance ensures that the local trajectory remains in $\mathcal{X}_f$, the decrease condition requires 
the terminal cost $V_f$ to decrease along the local closed-loop trajectory by at least the stage cost $\ell(x,\pi_f(x))$~
\citeAsm{5.9}{gruneNonlinearModelPredictive2017}.

The predictive mechanism and the geometric role of the terminal set are illustrated in \cref{fig:terminal-set-comparison}. 
While intermediate predicted states are required to remain in the 
safe state space $x_{i|k} \in \mathcal{X}$ for $i = 0, \dots, N-1$, the terminal constraint enforces $x_{N|k} \in \mathcal{X}_f$, where the local controller 
satisfies the Lyapunov decrease condition in \labelcref{eq:term-cost-decrease}~
\citeThm{2.19}{rawlingsModelPredictiveControl2020}.

For nonlinear systems, the terminal ingredients are typically designed by using a linearized discrete-time model, 
which can be obtained by linearizing the system dynamics around the equilibrium point $(x, u) = (\mathbf{0}, \mathbf{0})$, yielding
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:linearized-system}
    x_{k+1} = A x_k + B u_k,
\end{equation}
where $A = \frac{\partial f}{\partial x}(\mathbf{0}, \mathbf{0})$ and $B = \frac{\partial f}{\partial u}(\mathbf{0}, \mathbf{0})$ 
represent the system Jacobians~\citeSec{3.5}{rawlingsModelPredictiveControl2020}. 
Assuming $(A, B)$ is stabilizable and $(A, Q^{1/2})$ is detectable, the matrix
$P \succ 0$ is obtained as the unique stabilizing solution to the discrete-time
Algebraic Riccati Equation (DARE)~\citeEq{1.18,5.23}{rawlingsModelPredictiveControl2020,gruneNonlinearModelPredictive2017}:
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:riccati-equation}
    P = A^\top P A - A^\top P B \left(R + B^\top P B\right)^{-1} B^\top P A + Q.
\end{equation}

Using $P$, the local linear feedback law $\pi_f(x) = -K x$ is parameterized via the optimal Linear-Quadratic Regulator (LQR) gain~
\citeEq{1.18,5.24}{rawlingsModelPredictiveControl2020,gruneNonlinearModelPredictive2017}:
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:lqr-feedback}
    K = \left(R + B^\top P B\right)^{-1} B^\top P A,
\end{equation}
and the terminal cost function is specified quadratically as $V_f(x) = x^\top P x$.
Accordingly, the terminal set $\mathcal{X}_f$ is defined in this specific case 
as an ellipsoidal sub-level set of this cost function~
\citeSec{3.5}{rawlingsModelPredictiveControl2020}:
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:terminal-ellipse}
    \mathcal{X}_f := \left\{ x \in \mathbb{R}^{n_x} \ \middle|\ x^\top P x \leq \rho \right\}.
\end{equation}
By choosing the scaling parameter $\rho > 0$ sufficiently small, $\mathcal{X}_f$ 
satisfies both state and input constraints $\mathcal{X}$ and $\mathcal{U}$, 
while ensuring that the quadratic Lyapunov decrease dominates higher-order 
linearization errors of the underlying nonlinear dynamics.

\subsection{Recursive Feasibility and Closed-Loop Stability}
\label{subsec:closed-loop-stability}

Due to state and input constraints, as well as system dynamics, the optimal control problem (OCP) may be infeasible 
for arbitrary states $x \in \mathcal{X}$. 
Therefore, the MPC domain is restricted to the \emph{feasible set}, which is defined as~\citeSec{3.3}{gruneNonlinearModelPredictive2017}:
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:feasible-set-compact}
    \mathcal{X}_N := \left\{ 
        x_0 \in \mathcal{X} \;\middle|\; \exists (u_i)_{i=0}^{N-1} \in \mathcal{U}^N 
            \; : \; x_{i+1} = f(x_i, u_i) \in \mathcal{X} \; \forall i = 0, \dots, N-1, \, x_N \in \mathcal{X}_f
    \right\}.
\end{equation}
For any state $x_k \in \mathcal{X}_N$, solving \eqref{eq:mpc-problem} yields the optimal input sequence 
$(u_{i|k}^\star)_{i=0}^{N-1}$ and the corresponding optimal state sequence $(x_{i|k}^\star)_{i=0}^{N}$, 
where $x_{0|k}^\star = x_k$ and $x_{N|k}^\star \in \mathcal{X}_f$. 
Under the MPC policy $\pi_0$ from \labelcref{eq:mpc-feedback-policy}, 
the nominal closed-loop system yields the successor state $x_{k+1} = f(x_k, \pi_0(x_k)) = x_{1|k}^\star$.

\paragraph{Recursive Feasibility with Sequence Shifting.}
In the nominal closed-loop setup, an MPC formulation is said to be \textit{recursively feasible} 
if \labelcref{eq:mpc-problem} is feasible for $x_k \in \mathcal{X}_N$, resulting in a subsequent state $x_{k+1}$ 
that is also feasible. 
This can be demonstrated with the shifted candidate input sequence $(\tilde{u}_{i|k+1})_{i=0}^{N-1}$ 
constructed for the state $x_{k+1} = x_{1|k}^\star$, where the terminal controller $\pi_f$ is appended:
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:candidate-input-sequence}
    (\tilde{u}_{i|k+1})_{i=0}^{N-1} := \left( u_{1|k}^\star, \dots, u_{N-1|k}^\star, \pi_f(x_{N|k}^\star) \right).
\end{equation}
The corresponding predicted state sequence is given by
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:candidate-state-sequence}
    (\tilde{x}_{i|k+1})_{i=0}^{N} := \left( x_{1|k}^\star, \dots, x_{N|k}^\star, f(x_{N|k}^\star, \pi_f(x_{N|k}^\star)) \right).
\end{equation}
For $i = 0, \dots, N-2$, the pairs $(\tilde{x}_{i|k+1}, \tilde{u}_{i|k+1}) = (x_{i+1|k}^\star, u_{i+1|k}^\star)$ are feasible 
by construction. Additionally, for $i = N-1$, the input $\tilde{u}_{N-1|k+1} = \pi_f(x_{N|k}^\star)$ is admissible 
according to \labelcref{eq:term-input-constraint}, since $x_{N|k}^\star \in \mathcal{X}_f$.
The computed terminal state satisfies $\tilde{x}_{N|k+1} = f(x_{N|k}^\star, \pi_f(x_{N|k}^\star)) \in \mathcal{X}_f \subseteq \mathcal{X}$ 
due to the positive invariance of $\mathcal{X}_f$ in \labelcref{eq:term-positive-invariance}.
Thus, the control sequence $(\tilde{u}_{i|k+1})_{i=0}^{N-1}$ is feasible for $x_{k+1}$, 
which proves that $x_{k+1} \in \mathcal{X}_N$. Consequently, for all future time steps $k \ge 0$, 
the closed-loop state remains in the feasible set $\mathcal{X}_N$, 
which confirms both positive invariance of $\mathcal{X}_N$ under $\pi_0$ and recursive feasibility.

\paragraph{Lyapunov Decrease of the Optimal Value Function.}
As the shifted candidate input sequence is feasible for $x_{k+1}$, the optimal cost satisfies 
$V_N(x_{k+1}) \leq J(x_{k+1}, (\tilde{u}_{i|k+1})_{i=0}^{N-1})$. 
Subtracting the optimal cost at step $k$ from the candidate cost at time step $k+1$ yields:
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:cost-difference-derivation}
    \begin{aligned}
        &J \left( x_{k+1}, (\tilde{u}_{i|k+1})_{i=0}^{N-1} \right) - V_N(x_k) \\
        &\quad = \sum_{i=1}^{N-1} \ell(x_{i|k}^\star, u_{i|k}^\star) + \ell(x_{N|k}^\star, \pi_f(x_{N|k}^\star)) + V_f \left( f(x_{N|k}^\star, \pi_f(x_{N|k}^\star)) \right) \\
        &\qquad - \big[ \ell(x_k, u_{0|k}^\star) + \sum_{i=1}^{N-1} \ell(x_{i|k}^\star, u_{i|k}^\star) + V_f(x_{N|k}^\star) \big] \\[1.5ex]
        &\quad = -\ell(x_k, u_{0|k}^\star) + \underbrace{
            \big[ V_f(f(x_{N|k}^\star, \pi_f(x_{N|k}^\star))) - V_f(x_{N|k}^\star) + \ell(x_{N|k}^\star, \pi_f(x_{N|k}^\star)) \big]
        }_{\leq 0 \text{ due to \labelcref{eq:term-cost-decrease}}}.
    \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Applying the local Lyapunov decrease condition \labelcref{eq:term-cost-decrease} ensures that the bracketed term is 
non-positive~\citeAsm{5.9}{gruneNonlinearModelPredictive2017}. 
Together with $V_N(x_{k+1}) \leq J(x_{k+1}, (\tilde{u}_{i|k+1})_{i=0}^{N-1})$, the optimal value function satisfies the 
Lyapunov decrease condition along the nominal closed-loop trajectories~\citeLem{5.12}{gruneNonlinearModelPredictive2017}:
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:mpc-lyapunov-decrease}
    V_N(x_{k+1}) - V_N(x_k) \leq -\ell(x_k, \pi_0(x_k)), \qquad \forall x_k \in \mathcal{X}_N.
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Asymptotic Stability and Region of Attraction.}
To prove asymptotic stability of the origin, the optimal value function $V_N$ must satisfy the criteria of a 
Lyapunov function on the feasible set $\mathcal{X}_N$~\citeSec{2.3}{gruneNonlinearModelPredictive2017}. 
Given that $Q \succ 0$, the minimum eigenvalue satisfies $\lambda_{\min}(Q) > 0$. 
Together with $u^\top R u \geq 0$, this results in a strict lower bound on the stage cost \labelcref{eq:stage-cost}:
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:stage-cost-lower-bound}
    \ell(x, u) \geq x^\top Q x \geq \lambda_{\min}(Q) \|x\|^2 > 0, \qquad \forall x \neq \mathbf{0}.
\end{equation}
Consequently, this yields a negative upper bound on the Lyapunov decrease in \labelcref{eq:mpc-lyapunov-decrease}:
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:lyapunov-decrease-eigenvalue}
    V_N(x_{k+1}) - V_N(x_k) \leq -\ell(x_k, \pi_0(x_k)) \leq -\lambda_{\min}(Q) \|x_k\|^2 < 0, 
        \qquad \forall x_k \in \mathcal{X}_N \setminus \{\mathbf{0}\}.
\end{equation}
Furthermore, the optimal value function satisfies $V_N(0) = 0$ and the lower bound 
$V_N(x) \ge \ell(x, \pi_0(x)) \ge \lambda_{\min}(Q) \|x\|^2 > 0$ for all $x \in \mathcal{X}_N \setminus \{0\}$. 
Under the assumption that $V_N$ is continuous at the origin, $V_N$ is strictly positive definite on 
$\mathcal{X}_N$~\citeDef{2.13}{gruneNonlinearModelPredictive2017}.

In combination with the positive forward invariance of $\mathcal{X}_N$ established by recursive feasibility, 
$V_N$ defines a valid Lyapunov function. 
Therefore, the origin $\mathbf{0}$ of the nominal closed-loop system is asymptotically stable, and every 
trajectory initialized at $x_0 \in \mathcal{X}_N$ converges to the origin, making the feasible set $\mathcal{X}_N$ 
the \emph{region of attraction}~\citeThm{2.19,5.13}{rawlingsModelPredictiveControl2020,gruneNonlinearModelPredictive2017}.
