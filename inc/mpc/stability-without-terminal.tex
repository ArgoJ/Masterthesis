\section{Stability without Terminal Constraints}
\label{sec:stability-without-terminal}

Although terminal constraints simplify stability analysis, they restrict the feasible set $\mathcal{X}_N$ of 
the optimization problem and introduce additional complexity in finding suitable terminal ingredients \citeSec{6.1}{gruneNonlinearModelPredictive2017}. 
Alternatively, closed-loop stability can be demonstrated even without any stabilizing terminal conditions. 

In this setting, nominal asymptotic stability requires bounding $V_N(x_k)$ relative to the minimum stage cost
$\ell^*(x_k) := \inf_{u \in \mathcal{U}} \ell(x_k, u)$ \citeSec{6.2}{gruneNonlinearModelPredictive2017}:
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:general-stability-condition}
    V_N(x_k) \leq \gamma \ell^*(x_k),
\end{equation}
where $\gamma \geq 1$ quantifies the system controllability. 
Assuming a constant bound $\gamma$ across optimization horizons, 
this relation yields an explicit expression for the decrease factor $\alpha_N$ in 
\cref{eq:relaxed-mpc-stability} \citeRem{6.22}{gruneNonlinearModelPredictive2017}:
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:without-terminal-alpha}
    \alpha_N = 1 - \frac{(\gamma - 1)^N}{\gamma^{N-1} - (\gamma - 1)^{N-1}}.
\end{equation}

To guarantee a strict Lyapunov decrease ($\alpha_N > 0$), the prediction horizon $N$ must be chosen sufficiently large to satisfy 
\cite[Remark 6.22]{gruneNonlinearModelPredictive2017}:
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:without-terminal-N}
    N > 2 + \frac{\ln(\gamma - 1)}{\ln \gamma - \ln(\gamma - 1)}.
\end{equation}
Satisfying this lower bound guarantees global asymptotic stability for the nominal NMPC scheme
\citeSec{6.5}{gruneNonlinearModelPredictive2017}.

However, if the linear bound in \cref{eq:general-stability-condition} cannot be satisfied globally and 
is replaced by a general, nonlinear bound, exact asymptotic stability may be lost. Under these relaxed conditions, 
the MPC closed-loop system can still be shown to be semiglobally \textit{practically} asymptotically stable, 
meaning the system trajectories are guaranteed to converge to a sufficiently small neighborhood of the origin 
\citeThm{6.37}{gruneNonlinearModelPredictive2017}.
