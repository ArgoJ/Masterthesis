\chapter{Introduction}
\label{ch:intro}

The use of autonomous cyber-physical systems in safety-critical areas requires control architectures that combine high flexibility,
real-time capability, and strict safety guarantees. During the last decades, Model Predictive Control (MPC) has been well studied 
and is established as a method for constrained multivariable optimal control, offering deterministic satisfaction of the constraints. 
Furthermore, MPC provides well-understood stability guarantees for the closed-loop system, based on Lyapunov 
theory~\cite{gruneNonlinearModelPredictive2017,rawlingsModelPredictiveControl2020}. 
Even though numerical solvers become increasingly efficient, real-time execution of nonlinear MPC requires a significant amount of 
computational power, which is especially limiting for embedded systems.
To overcome this computational limitation neural control policies have proven to be a strong alternative. 
In particular, approaches based on imitative or Reinforcement Learning enable highly efficient computation during inference, 
due to a simple forward pass~\cite{kargEfficientRepresentationApproximation2020,hertneckLearningApproximateModel2018,hewingLearningBasedModelPredictive2020}.  
Furthermore, recent literature has increasingly focused on the synergistic synthesis of MPC and Reinforcement Learning~\cite{reiterSynthesisOfMPC2026}.

Despite their remarkable performance on high-dimensional and nonlinear tasks, the practical application of trained neural
control strategies in safety-critical areas remains severely limited. In industrial and public applications, deployment does not fail
due to lack of performance, but rather due to the absence of formal, verifiable guarantees regarding closed-loop stability,
forward invariance, and robustness~\cite{yangLyapunovstableNeuralControl2024}. 
Conventional neural networks function as black-box function approximators that generalize well on average,
but are known to be vulnerable to out-of-distribution shifts, domain violations, and 
adversarial perturbations~\cite{aggarwalNeuralNetworksDeep2023,zhangEfficientNeuralNetwork2018,goodfellowExplainingHarnessingAdversarial2015}. 

Given these vulnerabilities, the lack of formal guarantees is no longer just a theoretical problem, as lawmakers and regulatory agencies
around the world are shifting from general guidelines to binding technical requirements. 
Under the European Union's Artificial Intelligence Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689), autonomous and safety-critical control systems 
fall into the category of \textit{High-Risk AI}, which legally imposes strict requirements on risk management, deterministic robustness, and 
verifiable system reliability throughout the entire lifecycle~\cite{VerordnungEU20242024}. 
Crucially, while regulatory authorities establish these requirements, they deliberately do not prescribe any specific
mathematical or algorithmic methods for compliance. This work addresses this methodological gap by proposing a deterministic,
GPU-accelerated framework for training and formally certifying neural imitation controllers using learned neural Lyapunov functions.

\subsection*{Contributions of this Work}
\label{subsec:intro-contributions}

In contrast to prior work focusing on learning certified neural controllers from scratch, this thesis establishes an integrated framework 
for learning imitation policies from nominal MPC experts, followed by learning a corresponding Lyapunov candidate to formally verify the converged policy. 

A Dataset generated with an MPC expert is used to optimize a constrained policy architecture that enforces control input bounds 
$\underline{u} \le \pi_\theta(x) \le \overline{u}$ and satisfies the equilibrium constraint $\pi_\theta(x^\star) = u^\star$ by construction. 
The optimization penalizes deviations with state-dependent weights prioritizing accuracy near the origin, while promoting forward invariance 
using a one-step dynamics-aware loss on the unconstrained policy terms. Building upon the converged imitation policy, 
the policy is refined while simultaneously learning a structurally positive definite neural Lyapunov candidate $V_\phi(x)$.
The optimization objective incorporates a soft $\rho$-gated Lyapunov condition and state invariance loss, augmented by LiRPA 
bound propagation penalties as well as scaling and regularization terms to shape a verifier-friendly loss landscape.
To compute the gated loss, the sublevel bound $\rho$ is dynamically estimated along the domain boundary using $\ell_\infty$-Projected Gradient Descent (PGD). 
Simultaneously, adversarial mining using PGD actively searches for stability counterexamples, which are maintained in an age-tracked 
replay buffer and fed back into training to systematically eliminate localized condition violations.
For rigorous guarantees, a formal certification pipeline based on the complete $\alpha,\beta$-CROWN verifier utilizes a scaling and 
bisection search algorithm to soundly compute the maximal certified sublevel value $\rho^*$~\cite{yangLyapunovstableNeuralControl2024}. 
Formulating the stability criteria as a disjunctive logical specification over a punctured state space enables the solver to formally certify 
positive definiteness, regional forward invariance, and exponential Lyapunov decrease. The resulting closed-loop trajectories closely 
follow the MPC expert with high fidelity, while providing certified stability over a formally verified Region of Attraction.

\subsection*{Overview}
\label{subsec:intro-overview}

The remainder of this thesis consists of seven chapters, with \cref{ch:optimal-control} presenting the theoretical foundations for Lyapunov stability 
as well as for model predictive control (MPC) under constraints and closed-loop stability. This is followed by \cref{ch:ml}, which explains the 
fundamentals of neural networks, in particular multilayer perceptrons, efficient optimization, and backpropagation, as well as adversarial attacks 
using Projected Gradient Descent (PGD). \Cref{ch:formal-verification} provides an overview of the mathematical principles of formal neural network 
verification using $\alpha,\beta$-CROWN. Here, the incomplete linear bound propagation methods, which may yield inconclusive results due to relaxation errors, 
are presented, as well as a complete Branch-and-Bound (BaB) method using Lagrangian relaxations combined with incomplete verification methods. 
The proposed framework is detailed in \cref{ch:methods}, clarifying the imitation policy architecture and loss. It introduces the 
neural Lyapunov learning pipeline, describing the Lyapunov candidate architecture, the used loss formulations and data handling, as well as the verified 
sublevel set search. \Cref{ch:results} shows the experiments conducted on the linear double integrator and nonlinear cartpole as benchmarks. 
To this end, both the experimental setups and the resulting imitation and verification performance are presented for each system. 
\Cref{ch:discussion} discusses the results examining trade-offs and challenges. Finally, \cref{ch:conclusion} summarizes the essence
of this work and outlines further research recommendations for certified neural control.
