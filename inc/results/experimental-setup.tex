\section{System Modeling and Problem Formulation}
\label{sec:system-modeling}
In the following, both discrete-time system along with their state and input constraints are formulated, 
as well as their associated optimization problems. 
Subsequently, the shared implementation pipeline, including data generation, imitation learning, 
Lyapunov learning and the formal verification, as well as their corresponding hyperparameter configurations, are presented. 
All experiments are conducted on Linux hardware with an Intel Core i5 processor, an NVIDIA RTX 3060 GPU 
and 32\,Gb of ram. The entire pipeline is implemented in Python, relying on the core frameworks \texttt{PyTorch}~\cite{paszkePyTorchImperativeStyle2019}, 
$\alpha,\beta$-CROWN~\cite{wang2021beta,xuFastCompleteEnabling2021}, 
and \texttt{acados}~\cite{verschuerenAcadosModularOpensource2022}.

\subsection{Benchmark Systems}
\label{subsec:benchmark-systems}

\paragraph{Double Integrator.}
The double integrator is a simple second-order linear system. Therefore, it serves as an ideal baseline 
system in the learning and certification pipeline. Discretizing the continuous-time dynamics using the 
explicit Euler method with a sampling time of $\Delta t = 0.1\,\text{s}$ leads to the discrete-time state-space equation:
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:double-integrator}
    x_{k+1} = f_{\mathrm{DI}}(x_k, u_k) = \begin{bmatrix}1 & \Delta t \\
    0 & 1\end{bmatrix} x_k + \begin{bmatrix}0 \\ \Delta t\end{bmatrix} u_k,
\end{equation}
where $x_k = \begin{bmatrix}p_k & v_k\end{bmatrix}^\top$ is the state vector containing the position and velocity of the system, 
and $u_k$ denotes the applied force. 
The state, terminal, and input bounds are chosen as:
\begin{equation*}
    \underline{x} = \underline{x}_{\mathrm{MPC}} = \begin{bmatrix} -10 \\ -10 \end{bmatrix}, \quad
    \overline{x}  = \overline{x}_{\mathrm{MPC}} = \begin{bmatrix} 10 \\ 10 \end{bmatrix}, \quad
    \underline{x}_f = \begin{bmatrix} -5 \\ -5 \end{bmatrix}, \quad
    \overline{x}_f  = \begin{bmatrix} 5 \\ 5 \end{bmatrix}, \quad
    \underline{u} = -10, \quad
    \overline{u}  = 10.
\end{equation*}
For data generation, the optimal control problem in \labelcref{eq:mpc-problem} is solved with a prediction horizon of $N = 20$ stages. 
States and control inputs are penalized using the weighting matrices $Q = \operatorname{diag}(15, 1)$ and $R = 0.1$. 
The terminal weight matrix $P = \begin{bmatrix} 80.137 & 12.4 \\ 12.4 & 6.504 \end{bmatrix}$ is computed from the discrete algebraic Riccati equation (DARE), 
as described in \labelcref{eq:riccati-equation}. 


\paragraph{Cartpole.}
The inverted pendulum on a cart, commonly referred to as the cartpole system, is an underactuated nonlinear system. 
There are four states in the system: the cart position $p$, the cart velocity $v$, the pendulum angle $\varphi$, 
and the pendulum angular velocity $\omega$. 
Here, the discrete-time state vector is defined as $x_k = \begin{bmatrix}p_k & v_k & \varphi_k & \omega_k\end{bmatrix}^\top$,
and the control input $u_k$ is the force $F_k$ applied to the cart. 
Discretizing the continuous-time dynamics using the explicit Euler method with a sampling time of $\Delta t = 0.05\,\text{s}$ 
leads to the discrete-time state-space equation:
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:cartpole}
    x_{k+1} = f_{\mathrm{CP}}(x_k, u_k) =
    \begin{bmatrix}
        p_k + \Delta t \, v_k \\[4pt]
        v_k + \Delta t \, \frac{u_k + m l_p \omega_k^2 \sin(\varphi_k) - m g \sin(\varphi_k) \cos(\varphi_k)}{M + m - m \cos^2(\varphi_k)} \\[8pt]
        \varphi_k + \Delta t \, \omega_k \\[4pt]
        \omega_k + \Delta t \, \frac{-u_k \cos(\varphi_k) - m l_p \omega_k^2 
        \sin(\varphi_k) \cos(\varphi_k) + (M + m) g \sin(\varphi_k)}{l_p (M + m - m \cos^2(\varphi_k))}
    \end{bmatrix},
\end{equation}
where $M = 1\,\text{kg}$ is the mass of the cart, $m = 0.1\,\text{kg}$ is the mass of the pendulum, $l_p = 0.8\,\text{m}$ is the length of the pendulum, 
and $g = 9.81\,\text{m/s}^2$ is the gravitational acceleration. 
The corresponding state, terminal, and input bounds are defined as:
\begin{equation*}
    \underline{x} = \underline{x}_f = \begin{bmatrix} -2 \\ -10 \\ -3\pi \\ -10 \end{bmatrix}, \qquad
    \overline{x}  = \overline{x}_f = \begin{bmatrix} 2 \\ 10 \\ 3\pi \\ 10 \end{bmatrix}, \qquad
    \underline{u} = -80, \qquad
    \overline{u}  = 80.
\end{equation*} 
Instead of sampling initial states uniformly across the entire state space $\mathcal{X}$, trajectories are 
initialized from a smaller sampling domain $\mathcal{X}_{\mathrm{MPC}} := [\underline{x}_{\mathrm{MPC}}, \overline{x}_{\mathrm{MPC}}]$ with
\begin{equation*}
    \underline{x}_{\mathrm{MPC}} = \begin{bmatrix} -2 \\ -10 \\ -\pi \\ -10 \end{bmatrix}, \qquad
    \overline{x}_{\mathrm{MPC}} = \begin{bmatrix} 2 \\ 10 \\ \pi \\ 10 \end{bmatrix}.
\end{equation*} 
The cartpole MPC setup uses a prediction horizon of $N = 40$ stages, with weighting matrices 
$Q = \operatorname{diag}(10,\, 1,\, 10,\, 0.01)$ and $R = 0.5$. Furthermore, the 
terminal cost weight  is set to 
\begin{equation*}
    P =
    \begin{bmatrix}
        2112.121 & 957.461 & -3343.435 & -882.157 \\
        957.461 & 699.028 & -2562.405 & -682.313 \\
        -3343.435 & -2562.405 & 10833.55 & 2785.542 \\
        -882.157 & -682.312846 & 2785.542 & 737.426 
    \end{bmatrix}.
\end{equation*}

\subsection{Experimental Pipeline and Training Setup}
\label{subsec:experimental-pipeline}

\paragraph{Expert Data Generation.}
The closed-loop expert trajectories are computed using the open-source framework \texttt{acados}~\cite{verschuerenAcadosModularOpensource2022}, 
by solving the MPC optimization problem in \labelcref{eq:mpc-problem}. 
For both systems, a dataset $\mathcal{D}_\pi$ of 20{,}000 feasible trajectories is generated by uniformly sampling 
initial states from $\mathcal{X}_{\mathrm{MPC}}$. 
To ensure distinct data points, a state-wise minimum Euclidean distance filter is enforced between initial samples: 
$0.001$ for the double integrator, and $\begin{bmatrix} 0.01 & 0.001 & 0.001 & 0.001\end{bmatrix}^\top$ for the cartpole. 
Each closed-loop system is simulated over $k = 40$ steps for the double integrator and 
$k = 200$ steps for the cartpole to capture full stabilization and swing-up behaviors. 
The detailed solver configurations are shown in \cref{tab:app-acados-config}.


\paragraph{Imitation Policy Training.}
The imitation policy $\pi_\theta$ is based on the architecture defined in \labelcref{eq:policy-arch}, 
where an inner network $h_\theta$ is combined with state-wise clamping to ensure constraint satisfaction. 
An $80/20$ train-validation split is utilized to monitor generalization and drive the learning rate scheduler. 
Optimization is performed using Adam with a \texttt{ReduceLROnPlateau} scheduler that reduces the learning rate 
whenever the validation loss fails to improve for a given patience. 
To preserve imitation accuracy close to the origin, a state-dependent loss weighting $w_x(x) \in [w_{\min}, 1]$ is applied. 
Whereas only one hidden layer with 24 neurons is sufficient for the double integrator, 
the cartpole uses two hidden layers with 24 neurons each to be able to learn its nonlinearity. 
In addition, \cref{tab:hp-imitation} lists all imitation learning hyperparameters for both systems.

\paragraph{Neural Lyapunov Function Co-Training.}
The Lyapunov candidate $V_\phi(x)$ formulated in \labelcref{eq:lyapunov-candidate} combines an inner network $g_\phi$ 
with a positive definite quadratic term $(\varepsilon I + R_\phi^\top R_\phi)(x - x^\star)$. 
The matrix $R_\phi$ is initialized from the discrete algebraic Riccati equation (DARE) solution matrix $P$ 
of the linearized dynamics at the origin using the eigendecomposition $P = V \Lambda V^\top$ as $R_\phi = \Lambda^{1/2} V^\top$. 
For the double integrator, $R_\phi$ remains fixed throughout training, which eliminates the need for penalty terms related 
to the Frobenius norm and scaling. 
Conversely, for the cartpole, $g_\phi$ is parameterized with two hidden layers of 24 neurons each and $R_\phi$ is not fixed. 
To compute the LiRPA-condition loss $\loss_{\mathrm{L}}$, the state space $\mathcal{X}_V$ is partitioned into a uniform hyper-rectangular grid. 
The exclusion region $\mathcal{X}_{\mathrm{hole}}$, further defined in \cref{sec:formal-verification}, is used to avoid numerical issues near equilibrium.
Additionally, the co-training and falsification hyperparameters are listed in \cref{tab:hp-lyapunov}.

\paragraph{Formal Verification Setup.}
To formally certify the region of attraction and compute the maximal certifiable sublevel set $\mathcal{V}_{\rho^\star}$, 
the verification pipeline uses the scaling and bisection search in \cref{alg:search-and-bisect}, which relies on 
$\alpha,\beta$-CROWN~\cite{wang2021beta,xuFastCompleteEnabling2021}. 
As described in \cref{sec:formal-verification}, the verification is performed over the punctured state space 
$\mathcal{X}_{\mathrm{eval}} = \mathcal{X}_C \setminus \mathcal{X}_{\mathrm{hole}}$. 
All verifier settings and hyperparameter choices are listed in \cref{tab:hp-verification}.
