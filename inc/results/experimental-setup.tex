\section{System Modeling and Problem Formulation}
\label{sec:system-modeling}

For our experimental setup, two benchmark systems are selected: the double integrator and the cartpole. 
These systems provide a good balance between simplicity and complexity 
allowing us to certify the stability of the closed-loop system.


% ================================================================================
% Double Integrator
% ================================================================================
\subsection{Double Integrator}
\label{sec:double-integrator}

The double integrator is a simple second-order linear system; therefore, it serves as the ideal baseline 
system in the learning and certification pipeline. Discretizing the continuous-time dynamics using the 
explicit Euler method with a sampling time of $\Delta t = 0.1$, leads to the discrete-time state-space equation:

\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:double-integrator}
    x_{k+1} = f_{\text{DI}}(x_k, u_k) = \begin{bmatrix}1 & \Delta t \\
    0 & 1\end{bmatrix} x_k + \begin{bmatrix}0 \\ \Delta t\end{bmatrix} u_k
\end{equation}

where $x_k = \begin{bmatrix}p_k & v_k\end{bmatrix}^\top$ is the state vector containing the position and velocity of the system, 
respectively, and $u_k$ denotes acceleration.

The \textit{MPC setup} is formulated according to the problem in \labelcref{eq:mpc-problem} 
subject to the double-integrator dynamics in \labelcref{eq:double-integrator}. 
A prediction horizon of $N = 20$ steps is chosen, and the states and inputs are penalized using 
the weighting matrices $Q = \operatorname{diag}(15, 1)$ and $R = 0.1$.

% Cartpole

\subsection{Cartpole}
\label{sec:cartpole}

The inverted pendulum on a cart, commonly referred to as the cartpole system, is used as a nonlinear benchmark system in this work. 
There are four states in the system: the cart position $p$, the cart velocity $v$, the pendulum angle $\theta$, 
and the pendulum angular velocity $\omega$. 
Here, the state vector is defined as $x = \begin{bmatrix}p & v & \theta & \omega\end{bmatrix}^\top$,
and the input $u$ is the force $F$ applied to the cart. This leads to the following continuous-time dynamics of the cartpole system:

\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:cartpole-continuous}
    f_{\text{CP}}(x, u) =
    \begin{bmatrix}\dot{p} \\ \dot{v} \\ \dot{\theta} \\ \dot{\omega}\end{bmatrix} = 
    \begin{bmatrix}v \\ \frac{F + m l \omega^2 \sin(\theta) - m g \sin(\theta) \cos(\theta)}{M + m - m \cos^2(\theta)} \\ 
    \omega \\ \frac{-F \cos(\theta) - m l \omega^2 \sin(\theta) \cos(\theta) + (M + m) g \sin(\theta)}{l (M + m - m \cos^2(\theta))}\end{bmatrix}
\end{equation}

where $M = 1\,\text{kg}$ is the mass of the cart, $m = 0.1\,\text{kg}$ is the mass of the pendulum, $l = 0.8\,\text{m}$ is the length of the pendulum, 
and $g = 9.81\,\text{m/s}^2$ is the acceleration due to gravity.  
Discretizing the continuous-time dynamics using the explicit Euler method with a sampling time of $\Delta t = 0.05\,\text{s}$, 
leads to the discrete-time state-space equation:

\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:cartpole}
    x_{k+1} = f_{\text{CP},d}(x_k, u_k) = x_k + \Delta t f_{\text{CP}}(x_k, u_k)
\end{equation}

For the nonlinear benchmark, a prediction horizon of $N = 40$ stages is chosen. 
The system states and control inputs are penalized within the optimization framework of \labelcref{eq:mpc-problem} 
using the weighting matrices $Q = \operatorname{diag}(10,\, 1,\, 10,\, 0.01)$ and $R = 0.5$, respectively, 
subject to the dynamics defined in \labelcref{eq:cartpole}.
