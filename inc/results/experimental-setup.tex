\section{System Modeling and Problem Formulation}
\label{sec:system-modeling}
For our experimental setup, two benchmark systems are selected: the double integrator and the cartpole. 
While the double integrator allows for analyzing the pipeline on a low-dimensional reference system, 
the cartpole acts as a challenging benchmark to stress-test the co-training and falsification loop.
The hyperparameters reported below represent the most promising configurations identified during empirical tuning.

% Double Integrator

\subsection{Double Integrator}
\label{sec:double-integrator}

\paragraph{System Dynamics.}
The double integrator is a simple second-order linear system. Therefore, it serves as the ideal baseline 
system in the learning and certification pipeline. Discretizing the continuous-time dynamics using the 
explicit Euler method with a sampling time of $\Delta t = 0.1$, leads to the discrete-time state-space equation:
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:double-integrator}
    x_{k+1} = f_{\mathrm{DI}}(x_k, u_k) = \begin{bmatrix}1 & \Delta t \\
    0 & 1\end{bmatrix} x_k + \begin{bmatrix}0 \\ \Delta t\end{bmatrix} u_k
\end{equation}
where $x_k = \begin{bmatrix}p_k & v_k\end{bmatrix}^\top$ is the state vector containing the position and velocity of the system, 
respectively, and $u_k$ denotes acceleration.


\paragraph{Data Generation.}
The MPC setup is formulated according to the problem in \labelcref{eq:mpc-problem} 
subject to the double-integrator dynamics in \labelcref{eq:double-integrator}. 
Additionally, the stage state, terminal state and input constraint sets are given by 
$\mathcal{X} = [\underline{x}, \overline{x}]$, $\mathcal{X}_f = [\underline{x}_f, \overline{x}_f]$ and 
$\mathcal{U} = [\underline{u}, \overline{u}]$, with
\begin{equation}
    \underline{x} = \underline{x}_{\mathrm{MPC}} = \begin{bmatrix} -10 \\ -10 \end{bmatrix}, \qquad
    \overline{x}  = \overline{x}_{\mathrm{MPC}} = \begin{bmatrix} 10 \\ 10 \end{bmatrix}, \qquad
    \underline{x}_f = \begin{bmatrix} -5 \\ -5 \end{bmatrix}, \qquad
    \overline{x}_f  = \begin{bmatrix} 5 \\ 5 \end{bmatrix}, \qquad
    \underline{u} = -80, \qquad
    \overline{u}  = 80.
\end{equation}
A prediction horizon of $N = 20$ steps is chosen, while the states and inputs are penalized using 
the weighting matrices $Q = \operatorname{diag}(15, 1)$, $R = 0.1$ and $P = \left[[80.137, 12.4], [12.4, 6.504]\right]^\top$. 
Note that the terminal weight matrix $P$ was computed with DARE, as described in \labelcref{eq:riccati-equation}. 

Each MPC closed-loop trajectory is computed with \texttt{acados}~\cite{verschuerenAcadosModularOpensource2022} for a total of $k=40$ steps. 
Based on this setup, a total of 20{,}000 feasible trajectories are generated from uniformly sampled initial states, 
enforcing a minimum Euclidean distance of $0.001$.
The actual solver settings selected for this problem, can be seen in \cref{abs:acados-settings}. 


\paragraph{Control Policy Training.}
For the imitation policy as described in \labelcref{eq:policy-arch}, all hyperparameters used for the training 
are summarized in \cref{tab:hp-im-di}. Since this is a linear system, a compact network architecture is sufficient. 
An $80/20$ train-validation split is utilized to monitor generalization and drive the learning rate scheduler.
To achieve smooth convergence at later training stages, a \texttt{ReduceLROnPlateau} scheduler halves 
the learning rate whenever the validation loss fails to improve for five consecutive epochs. 
In order to maintain imitation accuracy especially at equilibrium, the state dependent weight is chosen to be 
$w_x(x) \in [0.5, 1]$.

\subsection{Cartpole}
\label{sec:cartpole}

\paragraph{System Dynamics}
The inverted pendulum on a cart, commonly referred to as the cartpole system, is used as a nonlinear benchmark system in this work. 
There are four states in the system: the cart position $p$, the cart velocity $v$, the pendulum angle $\varphi$, 
and the pendulum angular velocity $\omega$. 
Here, the discrete-time state vector is defined as $x_k = \begin{bmatrix}p_k & v_k & \varphi_k & \omega_k\end{bmatrix}^\top$,
and the control input $u_k$ is the force $F_k$ applied to the cart. 
Discretizing the continuous-time dynamics using the explicit Euler method with a sampling time of $\Delta t = 0.05\,\text{s}$ 
leads to the discrete-time state-space equation:

\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:cartpole}
    x_{k+1} = f_{\mathrm{CP}}(x_k, u_k) =
    \begin{bmatrix}
        p_k + \Delta t \, v_k \\[4pt]
        v_k + \Delta t \, \frac{u_k + m l_p \omega_k^2 \sin(\varphi_k) - m g \sin(\varphi_k) \cos(\varphi_k)}{M + m - m \cos^2(\varphi_k)} \\[8pt]
        \varphi_k + \Delta t \, \omega_k \\[4pt]
        \omega_k + \Delta t \, \frac{-u_k \cos(\varphi_k) - m l_p \omega_k^2 \sin(\varphi_k) \cos(\varphi_k) + (M + m) g \sin(\varphi_k)}{l_p (M + m - m \cos^2(\varphi_k))}
    \end{bmatrix}
\end{equation}

where $M = 1\,\text{kg}$ is the mass of the cart, $m = 0.1\,\text{kg}$ is the mass of the pendulum, $l_p = 0.8\,\text{m}$ is the length of the pendulum, 
and $g = 9.81\,\text{m/s}^2$ is the acceleration due to gravity.


\paragraph{Data Generation.}
For the nonlinear benchmark, a prediction horizon of $N = 40$ stages is chosen. 
The system states and control inputs are penalized within the optimization framework of \labelcref{eq:mpc-problem} 
using the weighting matrices $Q = \operatorname{diag}(10,\, 1,\, 10,\, 0.01)$ and $R = 0.5$, respectively, 
subject to the dynamics defined in \labelcref{eq:cartpole}.
