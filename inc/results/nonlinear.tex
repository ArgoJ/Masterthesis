\section{Nonlinear System Results: Cartpole}
\label{sec:results-nonlinear}

Compared to the linear double integrator, controlling the nonlinear cartpole system presents a significantly more challenging benchmark. 
The inverted pendulum dynamics are open-loop unstable around the upright equilibrium $\varphi^\star = 0$, 
and the strong nonlinear coupling terms between the cart acceleration and the pendulum states amplify small control deviations. 
To evaluate the approximation accuracy of the learned imitation policy compared to the MPC expert, the closed-loop tracking 
error trajectories over validation rollouts are illustrated in \cref{fig:cp-policy-error-bands}. 
\begin{figure}[htbp]
    \centering
    \input{tikz/results/cartpole/cp_policy_error_bands.tex}
    \caption[Closed-loop cartpole error bands]{
        Closed-loop trajectory tracking error between the learned imitation policy and the MPC expert for the cartpole system, 
        showing the error envelopes and the mean over time.}
    \label{fig:cp-policy-error-bands}
\end{figure}
As shown in \cref{fig:cp-policy-error-bands}, the unstable dynamics in the closed-loop system initially lead to temporary peak deviations 
due to minor cumulative differences. Nevertheless, the mean and median errors remain remarkably small throughout the rollout. 
Specifically, the mean angle error $|\Delta \varphi|$ stays below $0.13\,\text{rad}$ and the mean position error $|\Delta p|$ stays 
below $0.24\,\text{m}$, which corresponds to $6\,\%$ of the track width. 
Near the equilibrium after $3.5\,\text{s}$, all deviations contract toward zero, 
demonstrating reliable closed-loop stabilization to $\varphi^\star = 0$. 
Similar to the linear system, the imitation policy training loss shows steady convergence from initially $27.07$ to $3.183$, 
reflecting effective behavioral cloning of the MPC expert.
Exemplary individual trajectories comparing both expert and learned controllers are shown in \cref{app:cartpole-trajectories}.

The co-learning framework is initialized with the pretrained imitation policy and the DARE solution for the cartpole system. 
As visualized in \cref{fig:cp-cotraining-traces}, the training progresses over inner and outer epochs shows a converging behavior. 
The weighted condition loss $\loss_{\rho\mathrm{-gated}}$ starts at around $1$ and rapidly decreases to below $0.05$ 
after the $300$ inner epochs, while the number of mined counterexamples $N_{\mathrm{mined}}$ decreases from initially $46$ to $4$ in the final 
outer epoch. Furthermore, the estimated $\rho$-sublevel boundary $\tilde{\rho}$ increases from $177.831$ to a final value of $196.708$. 
Combined with the weighted $\loss_{\mathrm{ROA}}$ that decreases to $0.177$ and the number of mined counterexamples that decreases 
to $4$ demonstrates the effective learning process towards enlarging the region of attraction.

To analyze the remaining violations, \cref{fig:cp-final-counterexamples} illustrates the normalized states and violation 
magnitudes of the $4$ counterexamples mined in the final outer epoch. 
In contrast to the linear double integrator, in which the neural feature term provided significant local deformation, 
the feature term $|g_\phi(x) - g_\phi(x^\star)|$ contributes less than $1\,\%$ across the boundary bath $\mathcal{B}_\partial$, 
while the auxiliary term accounts for $99\,\%$.
Since the Lyapunov candidate remains predominantly quadratic in character, making it difficult to capture the unstable dynamics 
of the system outside the region around the equilibrium. Thus, the remaining counterexamples cluster at higher 
angles $\varphi$ and angular velocities $\omega$.

Although the loss metrics indicate a successful learning process and a growing region of attraction, 
the closed-loop performance of the co-trained policy degrades significantly outside the co-training state space 
compared to the initial imitation baseline. 
As illustrated in \cref{fig:cp-colearn-policy-error-bands}, the mean trajectory tracking error of the 
cart position increases to $|\Delta p| \le 0.62\,\text{m}$ compared to $|\Delta p| \le 0.24\,\text{m}$ for pure imitation. 
More critically, the peak position deviation reaches up to $7.5\,\text{m}$, which severely violates the 
allowable state bounds of $p \in [-2, 2]\,\text{m}$ considering the possible range of $4\,\text{m}$.
To illustrate how the co-trained policy exceeds the state bounds, \cref{fig:app-cartpole-trajectories} shows a 
comparison of the state and inputs for the expert, imitation and co-trained policy.

Following the co-training approach, the final pipeline step attempts to formally certify the region of 
attraction and compute the maximal certifiable sublevel set $\mathcal{V}_{\rho^\star}$ using $\alpha,\beta$-CROWN 
and the scaling and bisection search in \cref{alg:search-and-bisect}. 
However, for the nonlinear cartpole system, formal verification cannot prove the existence of any non-trivial 
sublevel set $\rho^\star > 0$ with the configuration specified in \cref{tab:hp-verification}. 
Even varying the verifier settings, such as the verification domain $\mathcal{X}_C$, the decrease rate $\kappa$, 
or the number of input partitions $p_{\mathrm{split}}$, does not result in a successful region certification.
A detailed discussion and analysis of these verification challenges is provided in \cref{ch:discussion}.
