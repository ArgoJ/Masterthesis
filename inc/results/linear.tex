\section{Linear System Results: Double Integrator}
\label{sec:results-linear}

Since the double integrator is a linear system, it serves as a good baseline to determine the performance of 
the proposed co-training and falsification pipeline. Considering the defined optimal value function of the double-integrator MPC in
\cref{fig:di-mpc-lyapunov}, there is a stable region around the origin. However, this region is 
only estimated based on the datasets' feasibility and lyapunov decrease using the $V = x^\top P x$ as a Lyapunov function. The dataset provides a 
practical foundation to learn a policy that is able to approximate the optimal control performance, while preserving the system's stability. 
To evaluate the approximation accuracy of the learned imitation policy, \cref{fig:di-policy-error-bands} shows the closed-loop absolute 
tracking errors over validation rollouts. 
Given the system bounds of $[-10, 10]$ for all states and control inputs, the policy achieves high tracking precision. 
The mean and median position deviations remain below $0.4\,\text{m}$ ($4\,\%$), while the corresponding velocity deviations 
remain below $0.5\,\text{m/s}$ ($5\,\%$). At the end of the trajectory, both state deviations approach zero. 
Even the peak worst-case position error remains below $0.81\,\text{m}$ ($8.1\,\%$), 
while the mean control force deviation peaks at only $1.17\,\text{N}$ ($11.7\,\%$) before decreasing to near zero. 
The imitation policy training loss converges from $23.787$ to $0.047$, as shown in \cref{fig:di-im-loss}.

With the learned imitation policy, the Lyapunov candidate can be learned in the co-training framework. 
Here, the loss $\loss_{\mathrm{total}}$ converges from $77.843$ to $1.37$, while the estimated $\tilde{\rho}$ also converges from $57.082$ to $613.4$. 
Based on the boundary samples used for the sublevel set estimation $\mathcal{B}_{\partial}$, the estimated feature term share 
$|g_\phi(x) - g_\phi(x^\star)|$ is $17.7\,\%$ and the positive-definite auxiliary term share 
$\left\|  (\varepsilon I + R_\phi^\top R_\phi) (x - x^\star)  \right\|_1$ is about $82.3\,\%$. 
This indicates that while the auxiliary term accounts for most of the magnitude and guarantees strict positive definiteness, 
the neural component provides the necessary local deformations to satisfy the decrease conditions. 
As illustrated in \cref{fig:di-cex-roa}, the number of mined counterexamples found during training, $N_{\mathrm{mined}}$, 
decreases from initially $141$ to $20$ in the final outer epoch, with a temporary increase to $299$ due to the aging buffer.
At the same time, the ROA loss $\loss_{\mathrm{ROA}}$ decreases from $11.312$ to $1.314$, 
which indicates an expanding region of attraction, with a simultaneous decrease in the number of counterexamples.

To demonstrate how the policy capabilities affect behavior in a closed-loop system after co-training, \cref{fig:di-colearn-policy-error-bands} 
illustrates the tracking errors of the co-trained policy relative to the MPC expert. 
Since the co-training algorithm described in \cref{subsec:training-procedure} modifies the policy parameters to satisfy 
the Lyapunov decrease conditions for the mined counterexamples and simultaneously enlarge the region of attraction, 
the max peak deviations increase moderately compared to the purely imitation learned policy. 
For the position and velocity the max peak deviation increase to $|\Delta p| \approx 1.655\,\text{m}$ and 
$|\Delta v| \approx 2.76\,\text{m/s}$, respectively. 
Nevertheless, the median deviations for position and velocity grow proportionally up to $|\Delta p| \approx 0.88\,\text{m}$ and 
$|\Delta v| \approx 0.856\,\text{m/s}$, while again converging to zero as $t \to 4.0\,\text{s}$. 
However, the peak control input deviation $|\Delta u|$ reaches $14.257\,\text{N}$, whereas its median stays below $3.4\,\text{N}$.
This demonstrates that the co-training pipeline trades a small amount of nominal imitation precision to 
achieve certified stability and expand the region of attraction without compromising task completion.

After training the candidate $V_\phi(x)$, the final pipeline step is to find a formally verified sublevel set $\mathcal{V}_\rho$ 
within which closed-loop stability can be certified. Given the configuration in \cref{tab:hp-verification}, the verifier identifies the 
formally verified $\rho^\star = 665.66$, as shown in \cref{fig:di-roa}. To provide geometric context, consider the state domain 
$\mathcal{X} = [-10,10]^2$, with a total area of $|\mathcal{X}| = 20 \times 20 = 400$. The certified region covers an area of $260.27$, 
corresponding to $65.07\,\%$ of $\mathcal{X}$, as estimated using Monte Carlo integration over $\mathcal{X}$.
For the nominal MPC value function, the quadratic sublevel set estimation yields an area of $183.22$, 
which corresponds to $45.81\,\%$ of $\mathcal{X}$, as visualized in \cref{fig:di-mpc-lyapunov}. 
These values provide a geometric reference for the certified sublevel set obtained after co-training.
The larger certified sublevel set is accompanied by a deviation from the nominal MPC behavior, as the policy adapts 
its control inputs to satisfy the Lyapunov decrease condition over a wider state domain.
