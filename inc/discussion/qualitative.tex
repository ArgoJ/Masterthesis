\section{Qualitative Discussion of Results}
\label{sec:discussion-results}

Supervised imitation learning based on nominal MPC trajectories serves as a strong inductive prior and 
enables compact neural policies to accurately reproduce the expert's behavior with low tracking errors in both benchmarks. 
However, since pure imitation learning lacks closed-loop stability guarantees, the co-training phase is designed to 
jointly refine the policy and learn a verifiable Lyapunov candidate. 
In contrast to synthesizing a neural Lyapunov candidate with the neural controllers from 
scratch~\cite{yangLyapunovstableNeuralControl2024}, 
the core idea of this work is to achieve formal verification while maintaining near-optimal closed-loop performance.

The results for the double integrator demonstrate that this pipeline can be successful for
imitation learning and formal verification for linear systems. 
Throughout co-training, the falsification loop systematically reduces the number of mined counterexamples, 
resulting in a formally certified region of attraction that is $42.1\,\%$ larger than the approximated MPC baseline. 
Key is that this expansion reflects an active trade-off, where the co-trained policy adapts its 
control actions away from near optimality.
Both the policy and the Lyapunov network are parameterized as compact ReLU Multi-Layer Perceptrons. 
Since ReLU networks yield piecewise linear input-output mappings, they are well-suited for linear 
relaxation and branch-and-bound verification in $\alpha,\beta$-CROWN. 
Furthermore, the neural feature term $|g_\phi(x) - g_\phi(x^\star)|$ accounts for an active $17.7\,\%$ share of the 
Lyapunov candidate when evaluated over the boundary states $\mathcal{B}_\partial$. 
This confirms that the learned candidate does not rely exclusively on the quadratic DARE auxiliary term, but rather that the 
neural network actively shapes the Lyapunov geometry to satisfy the decrease condition across an expanded domain.

In comparison, the cartpole benchmark demonstrates a deceptive convergence behavior 
that highlights critical challenges in the co-training of nonlinear systems. 
Although the condition loss $\loss_{\rho\mathrm{-gated}}$ and the number of mined counterexamples decrease 
during co-training, the closed-loop tracking performance of the resulting policy degrades, 
leading to substantial violations of the state constraints. However, these violating trajectories may have an initial state 
that lies outside the positively invariant, contracting sublevel set $\mathcal{V}_\rho$. 
Since the optimization primarily penalizes condition violations on the candidate landscape, 
the policy adaption can lead to a suboptimal control mapping.
This issue could potentially be mitigated by introducing a one-step state violation term as described in \labelcref{eq:dynamics-aware-loss} 
on a quasi-Monte Carlo shell batch sampled from $\mathcal{X}$. Nonetheless, incorporating an additional loss term makes the 
landscape more complicated and therefore harder to optimize. Furthermore, state generation consumes computational 
power, leading to slower training. 

Throughout the boundary batch $\mathcal{B}_\partial$, the neural feature term contributes less than $1\,\%$ to the 
Lyapunov candidate $V_\phi(x)$, leaving the function dominated by the auxiliary term. 
Consequently, the Lyapunov candidate remains mostly quadratic and cannot adapt its form to the nonlinear dynamics.  
As the counterexamples in \cref{fig:cp-cotraining-traces} show, condition violations 
occure at large pendulum angles $\varphi$ and angular velocities $\omega$, 
where the linearized DARE approximation starts to fail. 
To compensate for this structural mismatch, the optimization forces the policy to generate large control inputs. 
Furthermore, while the use of $\tanh$ activations for $V_\phi$ provides smooth representations, 
it leads to looser relaxations in CROWN compared to piecewise linear ReLU networks. 
Together, the degraded trajectory tracking and the loose bounds relaxations prevent the 
verifier from certifying a sublevel set $\rho^\star > 0$
