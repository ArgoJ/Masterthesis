\chapter*{Abstract}
\label{ch:abstract}

Neural control policies have emerged as an effective approach for controlling 
complex dynamical systems, enabling the imitation of Model Predictive Controllers. 
Nevertheless, their practical use in safety-critical applications remains constrained by the 
lack of rigorous closed-loop stability guarantees.
This thesis presents a framework for learning and formally certifying neural imitation controllers 
using learned Lyapunov functions. 
The proposed methodology combines supervised imitation learning from an expert MPC with falsification-guided 
co-training of the policy and a neural Lyapunov candidate, using mined adversarial counterexamples 
to refine the policy and Lyapunov candidate toward a verifier-friendly landscape. 
Local stability and forward invariance is then searched using 
the GPU-accelerated verifier $\alpha,\beta$-CROWN to formally verify the region of attraction. 
The framework is evaluated on both a double integrator and a nonlinear cartpole system.
