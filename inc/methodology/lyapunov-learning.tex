\section{Lyapunov Learning for Stability Certification}
\label{sec:lyapunov-learning}

To achieve formal stability with learned controllers, 
a Lyapunov candidate is trained to certify stability. 
A falsification-guided framework is used to find stability violations and 
therefore make the Lyapunov candidate a valid certificate for stability.

\subsection{Lyapunov Network Architecture}
\label{subsec:lyapunov-network-architecture}

Ensuring that the learned Lyapunov candidate is positive definite is crucial for stability certification.
To achieve this, a neural network architecture is designed to guarantee positive definiteness by construction, 
following the approach in \cite{lyapunov-stable-nn}. The architecture combines the flexibility of neural networks 
while ensuring the mathematical properties required for Lyapunov functions. 
Resulting from this design, the Lyapunov candidate is defined as:
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:lyapunov-candidate}
    V_{\theta}(x_k) = \left| f_{\theta}(x_k) - f_{\theta}(x^*) \right| 
        + \left\|  (\epsilon I + R^\top R) (x_k - x^*)  \right\|_1,
\end{equation}
where $f_{\theta} : \mathbb{R}^n \to \mathbb{R}$ is a neural network, $x_k \in \mathbb{R}^n$ is the current state, 
$x^* \in \mathbb{R}^n$ is the equilibrium, $\epsilon > 0$ is a small regularization constant, 
and $R \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times n}$ is a learnable matrix. 
While the first term is only positive semi-definite and may vanish away from the equilibrium, 
the second term guarantees strict positive definiteness $\forall x_k \in \mathcal{X} \setminus \{x^*\}$, 
as the matrix $(\epsilon I + R^\top R)$ is strictly 
positive definite by construction for any $\epsilon > 0$.



\subsection{Loss Formulation}
\label{subsec:lyapunov-formulation}

To ensure that the Lyapunov candidate described in \cref{eq:lyapunov-candidate} can be certified for stability, 
the loss function must comprise multiple components. 
It utilizes the ReLU function $[\cdot]_+$ for specific penalties, 
alongside a weighted mean to aggregate the sample losses.
Throughout the entire loss formulation, we consider a target sublevel value $\rho$, 
which is constrained by a lower bound $\rho_{\text{min}} > 0$ to ensure numerical stability.


\paragraph{Relative Lyapunov Decrease Loss} is designed to ensure that the Lyapunov candidate decreases along the system trajectories.
This is achieved by minimizing the relative difference between the Lyapunov candidate evaluated at the current state and the next state. 
For this purpose, the per-sample Lyapunov decrease term is defined as
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:lyapunov-decrease}
    e_V = V(x_{k+1}) - (1 -  \kappa) \cdot V(x_k) + \epsilon_m,
\end{equation}
where $\kappa \in (0, 1)$ is a hyperparameter that controls the desired rate of decrease, and $\epsilon_m \ge 0$ is a safety margin. 
To balance the optimization scale across different magnitudes of $V$, we normalize this violation to obtain the relative decrease loss:
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:rel-lyapunov-decrease}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{dec}} = \left[
        \frac{e_V}{|V(x_k)| + \epsilon_{\text{rel}}}
    \right]_+,
\end{equation}
where $\epsilon_{\text{rel}} > 0$ prevents division by zero.


\paragraph{Relative State Invariance Loss} penalizes trajectories that escape the training state space 
$\mathcal{B} = \{x \in \mathbb{R}^n \mid \underline{x} \le x \le \bar{x}\}$. 
Therefore, the relative one-step state-constraint violation loss for the next state $x_{k+1}$ 
across all dimensions $j = 1, \dots, n$ is formulated per sample as
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:rel-invariance}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{inv}} = \sum_{j=1}^{n} 
    \frac{\left[x_{k+1,j} - \bar{x}_j\right]_+ + \left[\underline{x}_j - x_{k+1,j}\right]_+}{\bar{x}_j - \underline{x}_j}.
\end{equation}


\paragraph{Combined $\rho$-Gated Condition Loss} is the sample-wise $\rho$-gated combination of 
the relative Lyapunov decrease loss from \cref{eq:rel-lyapunov-decrease} and the relative state invariance loss from \cref{eq:rel-invariance},
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:combined-condition-violation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{cond}} = 
        \mathcal{L}_{\text{dec}} + 
        \omega_{\text{inv}} \cdot \mathcal{L}_{\text{inv}},
\end{equation}
where $\omega_{\text{inv}}$ is a hyperparameter balancing the relative importance of the two loss components.
However, to apply the $\rho$-gate appropriately, $\mathcal{L}_{\text{cond}}$ is weighted for each sample using the soft sublevel weight
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:rho-gate}
    w_{\rho} = \operatorname{sigmoid} \left(
        s \cdot \frac{\rho - V(x_k)}{\max (\rho, \epsilon_{\text{rel}})}
    \right),
\end{equation}
where $s > 0$ denotes the gate sharpness. This smooth formulation ensures that the weights 
inside the $\rho$-sublevel are $\approx 1$, whereas the weights for states far outside of $\rho$ approach $0$. 
The weighted mean of the combined condition loss $\mathcal{L}_{\text{cond}}$ and $\rho$-gating weight $w_{\rho}$ 
results in the final loss formulation:
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:rho-gated-condition-loss}
    \mathcal{L}_{\rho\text{-gated}}
    = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^{N} w_{\rho}^{(i)} \cdot \mathcal{L}^{(i)}_{\text{cond}}}{\sum_{i=1}^{N} w_{\rho}^{(i)}}
\end{equation}
For this loss, samples are drawn from a counterexample and states mixed replay buffer, described in \cref{subsec:fals-guided-training}.


\paragraph{ROA Enlargment Loss} tries to enlarge the region of attraction by creating samples within an ellipsoidal 
boundary layer inscribed in the training space $\mathcal{B}$. The boundary candidates $x_{\text{ROA}}^{(i)}$ 
are generated using uniformly distributed directions scaled by a radius $r \in [0.6, 1]$, mapped to the dimensions of $\mathcal{B}$.
A batch of $M$ candidates is then evaluated using the following formulation:
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:roa-loss}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{ROA}} = \frac{1}{M} \sum_{i=1}^{M} \left[
        \frac{V(x_{\text{ROA}}^{(i)})}{\rho} - 1
    \right]_+.
\end{equation}


\paragraph{LIRPA-Condition Loss} makes the Lyapunov function more verifier-friendly 
by including formal violations of the Lyapunov decrease condition as defined in \cref{eq:lyapunov-decrease}, 
which are identified using the LIRPA framework \cite{lirpabook}. 
In order to compute these violations efficiently, only a filtered subset of regions is evaluated. 
This subset contains regions for which either the center $x_{\text{center}}$, lower bound $x_{\text{lower}}$ or 
upper bound $x_{\text{upper}}$ satisfies $V(x) \leq \rho$. 
LIRPA then provides a formal lower bound $s_{\text{lower}}$ on the 
signed Lyapunov decrease $-e_V$ for each of these regions. 
After evaluation, the LIRPA-condition violation for each region is computed as
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:relative-lirpa-condition}
    \mathcal{M}_{\text{L}} = \frac{[-s_{\text{lower}}]_+}{V(x_{\text{center}}) + \epsilon_{\text{rel}}}.
\end{equation}
The final LIRPA-condition loss is then computed as the weighted mean over all evaluated regions, 
where the weight is defined using an exponential function of the squared Euclidean distance between 
the region center $x_{\text{center}}$ and the equilibrium $x^*$:
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:lirpa-weight}
    w_{L} = \operatorname{exp} \left(
        - \alpha_{\text{L}} \cdot \lVert x_{\text{center}} - x^* \rVert^2_2
    \right),
\end{equation}
where $\alpha_{\text{L}} > 0$ is a hyperparameter controlling the decay rate of the weight.
Finally, the LIRPA-condition loss over $N_{\text{L}}$ regions is computed as
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:relative-lirpa-condition-loss}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{L}} = \frac{
        \sum_{i=1}^{N_{\text{L}}} w_{L}^{(i)} \cdot \mathcal{M}_{\text{L}}^{(i)}
    }{
        \sum_{i=1}^{N_{\text{L}}} w_{L}^{(i)}
    }.
\end{equation}


\paragraph{Lyapunov Scaling Loss} is introduced to ensure that the Lyapunov candidate does not collapse towards $0$. 
This is achieved by drawing a batch of $M$ quasi-Monte Carlo samples $x_V^{(i)} \in \mathcal{B}$ 
and specifying a target value $\ell_V$ for the logarithm of the average Lyapunov value 
$\ell_V = \log \left(\frac{1}{M} \sum_{i=1}^{M} V(x_{V}^{(i)})\right)$.  The scaling loss is then defined as
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:scaling-loss}
    \mathcal{L}_V = \left( \ell_{V,0} - \ell_V \right)^2.
\end{equation}
Note that $\ell_{V,0}$ is automatically set based on $\ell_V$ computed 
using the initial Lyapunov candidate. Also, the batch of samples $x_V^{(i)}$ is 
newly drawn after specified number of calls to prevent the Lyapunov candidate from overfitting 
to a fixed set of samples. 


\paragraph{Policy Scaling Loss} keeps the learned policy within a reasonable range 
to the output of the original imitaion controller $\pi_0$ based on the MPC teacher. 
For the generation of the $M$ sized sampled batch $x_{\pi}^{(i)} \in \mathcal{B}$, 
the quasi-Monte Carlo samling is used and also redrawn after a specified number of calls. 
These samples are then evaluated with the learned policy $\pi_V$ and 
the original imitaion controller $\pi_0$ by using the normed squared difference. 
The loss is then formulated as
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:policy-scaling-loss}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{\pi}} = \frac{1}{M} \sum_{i=1}^{M} \frac{\left(
        \pi_V(x_{\pi}^{(i)}) - \pi_0(x_{\pi}^{(i)}) 
    \right)^2}{\sum_{i=1}^{M} \left(
         \pi_0(x_{\pi}^{(i)}) 
    \right)^2}
\end{equation}


\paragraph{$R$-Matrix Loss} tries to prevent the collaps of the $R$ matrix in 
\cref{eq:lyapunov-candidate} towards a norm of $0$ during training. 
To achieve this, the Frobenius norm of the $R$ matrix is computed and compared to a target value $\ell_{R,0}$
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:r-matrix-loss}
    \mathcal{L}_R = \left( \ell_{R,0} - \lVert R \rVert_F \right)^2.
\end{equation}
Note that $\ell_{R,0} = \lVert R \rVert_F$ is automatically set based on the initial $R$ matrix.


\paragraph{Combined Loss} is the final loss function that combines all the previously defined weighted loss components. 
It is formulated as
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:combined-loss}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{total}} =
        \omega_{\text{cond}} \cdot \mathcal{L}_{\rho\text{-gated}} +
        \omega_{\text{ROA}} \cdot \mathcal{L}_{\text{ROA}} +
        \omega_{\text{L}} \cdot \mathcal{L}_{\text{L}} +
        \omega_V \cdot \mathcal{L}_V +
        \omega_{\pi} \cdot \mathcal{L}_{\pi} +
        \omega_R \cdot \mathcal{L}_R,
\end{equation}
where $\omega_{\text{cond}}, \omega_{\text{ROA}}, \omega_{\text{L}}, \omega_V, \omega_{\pi}, \omega_R$ are hyperparameters 
that balance the relative importance of the individual loss components.


\subsection{Falsification-Guided Training}
\label{subsec:fals-guided-training}

\paragraph{PGD Falsification} is utilized to identify counterexamples that violate the Lyapunov 
conditions and iteratively inject them into the counterexample pool $\mathcal{C}$. 
For this purpose, Projected Gradient Descent (PGD) is employed as a fast, 
heuristic falsifier, to search for states $x_{\text{adv}} \in \mathcal{B}$ 
that maximize the condition violation defined in \cref{eq:combined-condition-violation} 
and \cref{eq:rho-gate}. The per-sample PGD objective is defined as
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:pgd-objective}
    J_{\text{PGD}} = -w_{\rho} \cdot \mathcal{L}_{\text{cond}}.
\end{equation}
To perform the search, an iterative $L_\infty$-PGD is applied to candidate states recycled 
from the $\rho$-estimation replay buffer. 
The algorithm minimizes $J_{\text{PGD}}$ by iteratively updating the input states based on 
the sign of their local gradient with a constant step size $\alpha$ 
and projecting them back onto the bounds $\mathcal{B}$. 
To retain the strongest violations and prevent overshooting, the state achieving the 
minimum objective value across all $K$ iterations is kept for each candidate. 
Only states within the current $\rho$-sublevel set that exceed the violation tolerance 
and lie outside the origin exclusion region are retained as counterexamples.


\paragraph{Adversarial Replay Buffer} manages the sample distribution for the $\rho$-gated condition loss $\mathcal{L}_{\rho\text{-gated}}$. 
It maintains two distinct state pools: an explorative pool $\mathcal{S}$ and a counterexample pool $\mathcal{C}$. 

To concentrate the learning of the Lyapunov candidate on relevant regions, the explorative pool $\mathcal{S}$ 
is initially oversampled uniformly from the training region $\mathcal{B}$ every outer epoch. 
The final pool is then drawn based on a probabilistic sampling strategy 
that prioritizes states residing within the expanded boundary $m \cdot \rho$, using a scalar margin $m > 1$. 
For each candidate, a continuous sampling probability is computed via a sigmoid function. 
This assigns high probabilities to states inside the sublevel set and smaller probabilities to states further outside.

\begin{algorithm}[htpb]
    \caption{Probabilistic Explorative State Pool Resampling}
    \label{alg:state-pool-resample}
    \SetAlgoLined
    \KwIn{Lyapunov function $V_\theta$, current ROA estimate $\rho$, margin $m$, sharpness $\beta$, target size $N_x$}
    \KwOut{Updated state pool $\mathcal{S}$ of size $N_x$}

    \BlankLine
    Generate $4 N_x$ candidates: $\mathcal{X} \sim \mathcal{U}(\mathcal{B})$\;

    \BlankLine
    For all $x \in \mathcal{X}$, assign weight $w(x) \leftarrow \sigma \left(
        \beta \, \frac{m \cdot \rho - V_\theta(x)}{\max(\rho, 10^{-9})}
    \right) + \epsilon$\;

    \BlankLine
    $\mathcal{S} \leftarrow$ Sample $N_x$ states from $\mathcal{X}$ with probability $P(x) \propto w(x)$\;
\end{algorithm}
