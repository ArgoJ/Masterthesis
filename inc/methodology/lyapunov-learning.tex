\section{Lyapunov Learning for Stability Certification}
\label{sec:lyapunov-learning}
To establish formal stability guarantees for learned controllers, 
a neural Lyapunov candidate is trained alongside the policy.
A falsification-guided framework is used to find stability violations and 
therefore make the Lyapunov candidate a valid certificate for stability.

\subsection{Lyapunov Network Architecture}
\label{subsec:lyapunov-network-architecture}

Following \cite{yangLyapunovstableNeuralControl2024}, 
the Lyapunov candidate $V_\theta(x)$ is parameterized to be positive definite by construction:
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:lyapunov-candidate}
    V_{\theta}(x) = \left| g_{\theta}(x) - g_{\theta}(x^*) \right| 
        + \left\|  (\varepsilon I + R_\theta^\top R_\theta) (x - x^*)  \right\|_1,
\end{equation}
where $g_{\theta} : \mathbb{R}^{n_x} \to \mathbb{R}$ is a neural network, $x \in \mathbb{R}^{n_x}$ is the current state, 
$x^* \in \mathbb{R}^{n_x}$ is the equilibrium, $\varepsilon > 0$ is a small regularization constant, 
and $R_\theta \in \mathbb{R}^{n_x \times n_x}$ is a learnable matrix. 
While the first term is only positive semi-definite and may vanish away from the equilibrium, 
the second term guarantees strict positive definiteness $\forall x \in \mathcal{X} \setminus \{x^*\}$, 
as the matrix $(\varepsilon I + R_\theta^\top R_\theta)$ is strictly 
positive definite by construction for any $\varepsilon > 0$.
The complete architectural layout of $V_\theta(x)$ is illustrated in \cref{fig:neural-lyapunov}.

\label{subsec:lyapunov-formulation}

To ensure that the Lyapunov candidate described in \labelcref{eq:lyapunov-candidate} can be certified for stability, 
the loss function must comprise multiple components. 
It utilizes the previously introduced ReLU function $[\cdot]_+$ for one-sided penalties, 
alongside a weighted mean to aggregate the sample losses.
Central to the following loss terms is the estimation of the region of attraction (ROA). 
Throughout the training process, a target level $\rho$ is considered, leading to
the corresponding $\rho$-sublevel set within the training domain 
$\mathcal{X}_V := \left\{ x \in \mathcal{X}_\pi \mid \underline{x}_V \leq  x \leq \overline{x}_V \right\}$, 
defined as
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:sublevel-set}
    \mathcal{V}_\rho := \{ x \in \mathcal{X}_V \mid V_\theta(x) \le \rho \}.
\end{equation}
To prevent this certified region from trivially collapsing towards the origin during optimization, 
and to ensure numerical stability during normalizations, $\rho$ is strictly constrained 
by a lower bound $\rho_{\text{min}} > 0$.
Furthermore, the successor states $x^+_V = f(x, \pi_V(x))$ needed during loss computation are 
evaluated on-the-fly using the system dynamics and the policy. The latter may also be trained 
in the co-training setup and is thus referred to as $\pi_V$ hereafter.


\paragraph{Relative Lyapunov Decrease Loss} penalizes trajectories along which the Lyapunov candidate fails to decay. 
For each sample, the decrease violation is defined as
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:lyapunov-decrease}
    e_V = (1 - \kappa) V_\theta(x) - V_\theta(x^+_V),
\end{equation}
where $\kappa \in (0, 1)$ is a hyperparameter that controls the desired rate of decrease. 
To balance the optimization scale across different magnitudes of $V_\theta$, 
we normalize this violation to obtain the relative decrease loss:
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:rel-lyapunov-decrease}
    \loss_{\mathrm{dec}} = \left[
        \frac{e_V}{\max(|V_\theta(x)|, \varepsilon_{\text{rel}})}
    \right]_+,
\end{equation}
where $\varepsilon_{\text{rel}} > 0$ prevents division by zero. 
Note that the decrease normalization can be omitted depending on the training configuration.


\paragraph{Relative State Invariance Loss} penalizes successor states
leaving the Lyapunov training domain $\mathcal{X}_V $. 
To evaluate the relative one-step state-constraint violation, the scaling matrix 
$S_{\text{range}} = \diag(\bar{x}_V - \underline{x}_V)$ is utilized.
The loss per sample for the next state $x^+_V$ is computed as
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:rel-invariance}
    \loss_{\mathrm{inv}} = \left\| 
        S_{\text{range}}^{-1} \big[ x^+_V - \bar{x}_V \big]_+ 
    \right\|_1 + \left\| 
        S_{\text{range}}^{-1} \big[ \underline{x}_V - x^+_V \big]_+ 
    \right\|_1.
\end{equation}


\paragraph{Combined $\rho$-Gated Condition Loss} is the sample-wise $\rho$-gated combination of 
the relative Lyapunov decrease loss from \labelcref{eq:rel-lyapunov-decrease} and 
the relative state invariance loss from \labelcref{eq:rel-invariance},
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:combined-condition-violation}
    \loss_{\mathrm{cond}} = 
        \loss_{\mathrm{dec}} + 
        \omega_{\mathrm{inv}} \cdot \loss_{\mathrm{inv}},
\end{equation}
where $\omega_{\mathrm{inv}}$ is a hyperparameter balancing the relative importance of the two loss components.
However, to apply the $\rho$-gate appropriately, $\loss_{\mathrm{cond}}$ is weighted for each sample using the soft sublevel weight
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:rho-gate}
    w_{\rho} = \sigma \left(
        s \cdot \frac{\rho - V_\theta(x)}{\max (\rho, \varepsilon_{\text{rel}})}
    \right),
\end{equation}
where $s > 0$ denotes the gate sharpness. This smooth formulation ensures that the weights 
inside $\mathcal{V}_\rho$ are $\approx 1$, whereas the weights for states far outside of $\rho$ approach zero. 
Evaluating these terms on a mini-batch $\mathcal{B}_{\rho}$ drawn from the combined state pools 
$\mathcal{S} \cup \mathcal{C}$ (details in \cref{subsec:fals-guided-training}), 
the weighted mean of the combined condition loss results in the final loss formulation:
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:rho-gated-condition-loss}
    \loss_{\rho\mathrm{-gated}} = \frac{
        \sum_{\mathcal{B}_{\rho}} w_{\rho} \loss_{\mathrm{cond}}
    }{
        \sum_{\mathcal{B}_{\rho}} w_{\rho}
    }.
\end{equation}
The complete data flow, from evaluating the batch constraints to computing 
this final gated loss, is summarized in \cref{fig:rho-gated-loss}.

\paragraph{ROA Loss} tries to enlarge the region of attraction by uniformly drawing a batch 
$\mathcal{B}_{\text{ROA}}$ from $\mathcal{X}_V \setminus \xi \mathcal{X}_V$, 
where $\xi \in [0, 1)$ is a scaling factor defining the inner boundary of the shell, 
as shown in \cref{fig:box-shell}.
The loss is then evaluated over these candidates 
using \cite{yangLyapunovstableNeuralControl2024} 
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:roa-loss}
    \loss_{\mathrm{ROA}} = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{B}_{\text{ROA}}|} \sum_{x \in \mathcal{B}_{\text{ROA}}} \ln \left(
        \left[
            \frac{V_\theta(x)}{\rho} - 1
        \right]_+ + 1
    \right).
\end{equation}
Consequently, the loss function penalizes shell samples that fall outside the current $\rho$ subsets. 
As the estimated region of attraction expands, fewer samples are penalized, 
causing the loss to decrease.

\paragraph{LIRPA-Condition Loss} makes the Lyapunov function more verifier-friendly 
by penalizing formal violations of the Lyapunov decrease condition as defined in \labelcref{eq:lyapunov-decrease}. 
These violations are identified using the LIRPA framework \cite{xuFastCompleteEnabling2021}.
To reduce the computational cost during training, LIRPA is applied only to a 
heuristic subset of regions. Specifically, a hyper-rectangular cell is selected 
if at least one of its $2^{n_x}$ corners, or optionally its center point $x_{\mathrm{center}}$, 
lies within the sublevel set $\mathcal{V}_\rho$ (i.e., $\min(V(x)) \leq \rho$ evaluated over these points).
For each selected region, LIRPA then provides a formal lower bound $e_{V,\mathrm{lower}}$ on the 
signed Lyapunov decrease $-e_V$. 
After evaluation, the LIRPA-condition violation for each region is computed as
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:relative-lirpa-condition}
    \mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{L}} = \frac{
        \ln ([-e_{V,\mathrm{lower}}]_+ + 1)
    }{
        \max(V_\theta(x_{\mathrm{center}}), \varepsilon_{\mathrm{rel}})
    }.
\end{equation}
The final LIRPA-condition loss is then computed as the weighted mean over all evaluated regions, 
where the weight is defined using an exponential function of the squared Euclidean distance between 
the region center $x_{\mathrm{center}}$ and the equilibrium $x^*$:
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:lirpa-weight}
    w_{\mathrm{L}} = \exp \left(
        - \alpha_{\mathrm{L}} \cdot \lVert x_{\mathrm{center}} - x^* \rVert^2_2
    \right),
\end{equation}
where $\alpha_{\mathrm{L}} > 0$ is a hyperparameter controlling the decay rate of the weight.
Finally, the LIRPA-condition loss over $N_{\mathrm{L}}$ regions is computed as
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:relative-lirpa-condition-loss}
    \loss_{\mathrm{L}} = \frac{
        \sum_{i=1}^{N_{\mathrm{L}}} w_{L}^{(i)} \cdot \mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{L}}^{(i)}
    }{
        \sum_{i=1}^{N_{\mathrm{L}}} w_{L}^{(i)}
    }.
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Lyapunov Scaling Loss} prevents numerical collapse to zero 
by penalizing deviations from the initial average magnitude over a 
quasi-Monte Carlo batch $\mathcal{B}_V \subset \mathcal{X}_V$, 
sampled via a low-discrepancy Sobol sequence \cite{joeConstructingSobolSequences2008}, as illustrated in \cref{fig:sobol-sampling}.
The logarithm of the mean Lyapunov value over this batch is defined as
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:mean-lyapunov-log}
    \ell_V = \ln \left( \frac{1}{|\mathcal{B}_V|} \sum_{x \in \mathcal{B}_V} V_\theta(x) \right),
\end{equation}
leading to the scaling loss formulation
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:scaling-loss}
    \loss_V = \left( \ell_{V,0} - \ell_V \right)^2.
\end{equation}
The reference value $\ell_{V,0}$ is determined once using the initial 
Lyapunov candidate prior to training. To prevent the Lyapunov candidate from 
overfitting to a static point cloud, a new batch $\mathcal{B}_V$ is resampled from $\mathcal{X}_V$ 
with a specified probability at each optimization step.

\end{figure}


\paragraph{Policy Scaling Loss} encourages the learned policy $\pi_V$ to remain close 
to the output of the original imitation controller $\pi$, which is based on the MPC teacher. 
A quasi-Monte Carlo batch $\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{pol}} \subset \mathcal{X}_V$ is sampled using a 
low-discrepancy Sobol sequence \cite{joeConstructingSobolSequences2008}. 
To prevent overfitting to a static set of states, this batch also is resampled from $\mathcal{X}_V$ 
with a given probability each optimization step. 
The normalized deviation between the learned policy and the imitation controller over this batch is formulated as
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:policy-scaling-loss}
    \loss_{\mathrm{pol}} = \frac{
        \sum_{x \in \mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{pol}}} \lVert \pi_V(x) - \pi(x) \rVert_2^2
    }{
        \sum_{x \in \mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{pol}}} \lVert \pi(x) \rVert_2^2
    }
\end{equation}


\paragraph{$R$-Matrix Loss} regularizes the learnable matrix $R_\theta$ by penalizing deviations 
of its Frobenius norm from its initial value $\ell_{R,0}$:
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:r-matrix-loss}
    \loss_R = \left( \ell_{R,0} - \lVert R \rVert_F \right)^2.
\end{equation}
However, $\ell_{R,0} = \lVert R_\theta \rVert_F$ is automatically computed, 
based on the initial $R_{\theta,0}$ matrix.


\paragraph{Overall Lyapunov Loss} represents the combined objective function, 
summing all previously defined weighted components, given by 
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:combined-loss}
    \loss_{\mathrm{total}} = \sum_{m}
        \omega_m \loss_m
\end{equation}
where the weights $\omega_m > 0$, with $m \in \{ \rho\mathrm{-gated},\;\mathrm{ROA},\;\mathrm{L},\;V,\;\mathrm{pol},\;R \}$ are 
hyperparameters that balance the relative importance of the individual loss components. 



\subsection{Falsification-Guided Training}
\label{subsec:fals-guided-training}
Relying solely on uniform state sampling is not sufficient to reliably detect violations of the Lyapunov condition, 
especially in higher-dimensional state spaces \cite{yangLyapunovstableNeuralControl2024}. To overcome this problem, falsification-guided training 
actively searches for adversarial counterexamples and incorporates them into the training distribution as described in 
\cref{subsec:pgd-lyapunov-control}. This is made possible by an adversarial replay buffer that reconciles the exploration of the 
estimated attractor region with the targeted exploitation of known error modes.


\paragraph{PGD Falsification} is utilized to identify counterexamples that violate the Lyapunov 
conditions and iteratively inject them into the counterexample pool $\mathcal{C}$. 
Specifically, an $\ell_\infty$-Projected Gradient Descent (PGD) is employed 
to search for adversarial states $x_{\mathrm{adv}} \in \mathcal{X}_V$ 
that maximize the condition violation defined in \labelcref{eq:combined-condition-violation} 
and \labelcref{eq:rho-gate}. The per-sample PGD objective is defined as
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:pgd-objective}
    \mathcal{J}(x; \theta) = w_{\rho}(x) \loss_{\mathrm{cond}}(x).
\end{equation}
The adversarial search is initialized using the explorative pool $\mathcal{S}$, 
with up to $25\%$ of the batch supplemented by existing counterexamples to encourage local refinement.
Starting from an initial candidate $x_{\mathrm{adv}}^{(0)}$, the algorithm maximizes $\mathcal{J}$ 
by iteratively updating candidate states along the sign of their loss gradient:
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:pgd-update-lyapunov}
    x_{\mathrm{adv}}^{(j+1)} \leftarrow \Pi_{\mathcal{X}_V} \left(
        x_{\mathrm{adv}}^{(j)} + \alpha_{\mathrm{PGD}} \operatorname{sign} \left(
            \nabla_{x_{\mathrm{adv}}^{(j)}} \mathcal{J}\left(x_{\mathrm{adv}}^{(j)}; \theta\right)
        \right)
    \right),
\end{equation}
where $\alpha_{\mathrm{PGD}} > 0$ denotes the step size and $\Pi_{\mathcal{X}_V}(\cdot)$ projects the updated 
sample back onto the training domain bounds $\mathcal{X}_V$. 
To retain the strongest violations and mitigate the effects of overshooting, the state yielding the 
maximum objective value across all $I$ iterations is preserved for each candidate. 
Ultimately, only states strictly within $\mathcal{V}_\rho$ that exceed the violation tolerance 
and lie outside the origin exclusion region are retained as counterexamples.


\paragraph{Adversarial Replay Buffer} manages the sample distribution for the $\rho$-gated condition loss $\loss_{\rho\mathrm{-gated}}$. 
It maintains two distinct state pools: an explorative pool $\mathcal{S}$ and a counterexample pool $\mathcal{C}$. \newline
As outlined in \cref{alg:state-pool-resample}, the explorative state pool $\mathcal{S}$ is repopulated 
at each outer epoch to balance global exploration with local refinement. 
First, candidate states $\mathcal{B}_{\mathcal{S}}$ are oversampled uniformly from the training domain $\mathcal{X}_V$. 
From these candidates, $N_{\mathcal{S}}$ states are probabilistically sampled based on a sigmoid weighting function 
centered around an expanded sublevel boundary $m \cdot \rho$ (with margin $m > 1$). 
This weighting smoothly penalizes states further outside the estimated region of attraction, 
significantly reducing their likelihood of being selected.

\begin{algorithm}[htpb]
    \caption{Probabilistic Explorative State Pool Resampling}
    \label{alg:state-pool-resample}

    \KwIn{Lyapunov function $V_\theta$, current ROA estimate $\rho$, margin $m$, sharpness $s$, target size $N_{\mathcal{S}}$}
    \KwOut{Updated state pool $\mathcal{S}$ of size $N_{\mathcal{S}}$}

    \BlankLine
    Generate $4 N_{\mathcal{S}}$ candidates: $\mathcal{B}_{\mathcal{S}} \sim \mathcal{U}(\mathcal{X}_V)$\;

    \BlankLine
    For all $x \in \mathcal{B}_{\mathcal{S}}$, assign weight $w(x) \leftarrow \sigma \left(
        s \, \frac{m \cdot \rho - V_\theta(x)}{\max(\rho, 10^{-9})}
    \right) + \varepsilon$\;

    \BlankLine
    $\mathcal{S} \leftarrow$ Sample $N_{\mathcal{S}}$ states from $\mathcal{B}_{\mathcal{S}}$ with probability $P(x) \propto w(x)$\;
\end{algorithm}

The counterexample pool $\mathcal{C}$ stores the critical violations identified during the PGD falsification step. 
A age-tracking mechanism limits sample lifespan, preventing overfitting outdated violations.
Each injected counterexample is assigned an initial age $\tau = 0$, which is incremented after every subsequent falsification phase. 
Once a counterexample reaches the maximum age $\tau_{\max}$, it is removed from the pool. 
Consequently, the condition loss continuously penalizes recent violations while discarding resolved edge cases as the Lyapunov candidate evolves.



\subsection{\texorpdfstring{$\rho$}{ρ}-Sublevel Estimation}
\label{subsec:rho-sublevel-estim}
To evaluate the $\rho$-gated condition loss in \labelcref{eq:rho-gated-condition-loss}, the target sublevel bound $\rho$ 
must be continuously estimated throughout training as the Lyapunov candidate $V_\theta(x)$ evolves. 
Since the goal is to identify the largest sublevel set $\mathcal{V}_\rho := \{ x \in \mathcal{X}_V \mid V_\theta(x) \le \rho \}$ 
that remains within the training domain $\mathcal{X}_V$, the sublevel set reaches the boundary of the domain at 
its maximum admissible value. 
Therefore, the theoretical upper bound for $\rho$ is given by the minimum value of $V\theta(x)$ on the boundary:
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:rho-boundary-definition}
    \rho_{\mathrm{max}} = \min_{x \in \partial \mathcal{X}_V} V_\theta(x).
\end{equation}

Following the boundary-evaluation principle established by \cite{yangLyapunovstableNeuralControl2024}, evaluating $V_\theta(x)$ 
along the boundary $\partial \mathcal{X}_V$ is sufficient to estimate $\rho_{\mathrm{max}}$ without searching the entire domain. 
However, because $\partial \mathcal{X}_V$ can be a high-dimensional surface and $V_\theta(x)$ defines a non-convex landscape, 
simple uniform sampling on the boundary may fail to identify low-valued boundary points. 
Therefore, Projected Gradient Descent (PGD) is employed to search for local minima of $V\theta(x)$ along the boundary.

Starting from uniform boundary seeds $\mathcal{B}_{\partial} \sim \mathcal{U}(\partial \mathcal{X}_V)$, 
PGD \emph{iteratively minimizes} $V_\theta(x)$ along the negative gradient.
To maintain candidate states on the hyper-rectangular boundary surface $\partial\mathcal{X}_V$, 
the projection operator $\Pi_{\partial \mathcal{X}_V}$ projects the updated states onto the domain bounds 
while keeping the active face-defining coordinate fixed at its boundary value. 
For an unconstrained gradient step $z$ originating from a boundary face along dimension $j$ with active limit 
$c_j \in \{\underline{x}_j, \overline{x}_j\}$, the projection is defined element-wise for $i = 1, \dots, n_x$ as:
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:boundary-projection}
    \left[ \Pi_{\partial \mathcal{X}_V}(z) \right]_i = 
    \begin{cases}
        c_j, & \text{if } i = j, \\
        \clamp \left(z_i, \underline{x}_i, \overline{x}_i\right), & \text{if } i \neq j.
    \end{cases}
\end{equation}

To encourage the region of attraction to expand during co-training, the estimated boundary minimum is intentionally 
overestimated by applying a growth factor $\gamma > 1$:
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:rho-estimation}
    \tilde{\rho} = \max \left( 
        \rho_{\text{min}}, \gamma \cdot \min_{x \in \mathcal{B}_{\partial}} V_\theta(x) 
    \right).
\end{equation}

By targeting a value for $\tilde{\rho}$ slightly above the currently safe maximum, the formulation deliberately 
leads to small violations near the boundary. These violations are subsequently captured by the adversarial replay buffer, 
pushing the optimization to expand the sublevel set. 
Finally, to reduce fluctuations caused by changes in the estimated boundary minimum during training, 
the gating bound $\rho$ is updated using an exponential moving average (EMA):
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:rho-ema}
    \rho \leftarrow \upsilon \rho + (1 - \upsilon) \tilde{\rho},
\end{equation}
where $\upsilon \in [0, 1)$ denotes the EMA decay rate.



\subsection{Overall Training Procedure}
\label{subsec:training-procedure}

The complete falsification-guided training procedure is illustrated in
\cref{fig:train-flow}. It consists of an outer loop for data generation and an
inner loop for network optimization. \newline
At the beginning of each outer epoch, boundary samples $\mathcal{B}_{\partial}$ are used to estimate 
the current sublevel bound $\rho$, and the ROA shell batch $\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{ROA}}$ is redrawn. 
Based on the updated estimation of $\rho$, the active LiRPA regions are selected and PGD-based falsification 
is performed within the corresponding sublevel set. The resulting counterexamples are added to $\mathcal{C}$, 
after which the explorative state pool $\mathcal{S}$ is resampled. \newline
The inner loop is executed $K_{\mathrm{steps}}$ times per outer epoch. At each step,  
a mini-batch $\mathcal{B}_{\rho}$ is sampled from $\mathcal{S} \cup \mathcal{C}$ and 
evaluated together with the active LiRPA regions, the ROA shell batch, and Sobol batches
$\mathcal{B}_V$ and $\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{pol}}$. 
Their corresponding loss terms are combined into $\loss_{\mathrm{total}}$, which is minimized by
backpropagation with respect to the trainable network parameters. 
Repeating this procedure over $N_{\mathrm{outer}}$ epochs progressively reduces
Lyapunov-condition violations and enlarges the estimated certified region of
attraction.

    \input{tikz/methods/train_flow.tex}

After completion of the training procedure, the optimized parameters of the policy $\pi_V$ and the Lyapunov candidate $V_\theta$ 
yield a candidate control-Lyapunov pair. 
However, while falsification-guided adversarial training seeks to eliminate counterexamples over the trained state domain, 
it does not guarantee the complete absence of stability violations across the continuous state space. 
To transition from empirical validation to a formal stability proof, the learned candidate pair $(\pi_V, V_\theta)$ 
is subsequently subjected to formal neural network verification, as detailed in the following section.
