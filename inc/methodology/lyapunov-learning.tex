\section{Lyapunov Learning for Stability Certification}
\label{sec:lyapunov-learning}

To achieve formal stability with learned controllers, 
a Lyapunov candidate is learned to certify stability. 
For this purpose, a neural network, as described in \cref{subsec:mlp}, 
is trained to approximate a Lyapunov candidate. 
The Counterexample-Guided Inductive Synthesis (\textit{CEGIS}) framework is used to find 
stability violations and therefore make the Lyapunov candidate a valid certificate for stability.

\subsection{Lyapunov Network Architecture}
\label{subsec:lyapunov-network-architecture}

Ensuring that the learned Lyapunov candidate is positive definite is crucial for stability certification.
To achieve this, a neural network architecture is designed to guarantee positive definiteness by construction, 
following the approach in \cite{lyapunov-stable-nn}. The architecture combines the flexibility of neural networks 
while ensuring the mathematical properties required for Lyapunov functions. 
Resulting from this design, the Lyapunov candidate is defined as:
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:lyapunov-candidate}
    V_{\phi}(x_k) = \left| f_{\phi}(x_k) - f_{\phi}(x^*) \right| 
        + \left\|  (\epsilon I + R^\top R) (x_k - x^*)  \right\|_1,
\end{equation}
where $f_{\phi} : \mathbb{R}^n \to \mathbb{R}$ is a neural network, $x_k \in \mathbb{R}^n$ is the current state, 
$x^* \in \mathbb{R}^n$ is the equilibrium, $\epsilon > 0$ is a small regularization constant, 
and $R \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times n}$ is a learnable matrix. 
While the first term is only positive semi-definite and may vanish away from the equilibrium, 
the second term guarantees strict positive definiteness $\forall x_k \in \mathcal{X} \setminus \{x^*\}$, 
as the matrix $(\epsilon I + R^\top R)$ is strictly 
positive definite by construction for any $\epsilon > 0$.



\subsection{Loss Formulation and CEGIS Training}
\label{subsec:lyapunov-formulation}

To ensure that the Lyapunov candidate described in \cref{eq:lyapunov-candidate} can be certified for stability, 
the loss function must comprise multiple components. 
It utilizes the ReLU function $[\cdot]_+$ for specific penalties, 
alongside a weighted mean to aggregate the sample losses.
Throughout the entire loss formulation, we consider a target sublevel value $\rho$, 
which is constrained by a lower bound $\rho_{\text{min}} > 0$ to ensure numerical stability.


\paragraph{Relative Lyapunov Decrease Loss} is designed to ensure that the Lyapunov candidate decreases along the system trajectories.
This is achieved by minimizing the relative difference between the Lyapunov candidate evaluated at the current state and the next state. 
For this purpose, the per-sample Lyapunov decrease term is defined as
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:lyapunov-decrease}
    e_{V}(x_k, x_{k+1}) = V(x_{k+1}) - (1 -  \kappa) \cdot V(x_k) + \epsilon_m,
\end{equation}
where $\kappa \in (0, 1)$ is a hyperparameter that controls the desired rate of decrease, and $\epsilon_m \ge 0$ is a safety margin. 
To balance the optimization scale across different magnitudes of $V$, we normalize this violation to obtain the relative decrease loss:
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:rel-lyapunov-decrease}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{dec}} = \left[
        \frac{e_{V}(x_k, x_{k+1})}{|V(x_k)| + \epsilon_{\text{rel}}}
    \right]_+,
\end{equation}
where $\epsilon_{\text{rel}} > 0$ prevents division by zero.


\paragraph{Relative State Invariance Loss} penalizes trajectories that escape the training state space 
$\mathcal{B} = \{x \in \mathbb{R}^n \mid \underline{x} \le x \le \bar{x}\}$. 
Therefore, the relative one-step state-constraint violation loss for the next state $x_{k+1}$ 
across all dimensions $j = 1, \dots, n$ is formulated per sample as
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:rel-invariance}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{inv}} = \sum_{j=1}^{n} 
    \frac{\left[x_{k+1,j} - \bar{x}_j\right]_+ + \left[\underline{x}_j - x_{k+1,j}\right]_+}{\bar{x}_j - \underline{x}_j}.
\end{equation}


\paragraph{Combined $\rho$-Gated Condition Loss} is the sample-wise $\rho$-gated combination of 
the relative Lyapunov decrease loss from \cref{eq:rel-lyapunov-decrease} and the relative state invariance loss from \cref{eq:rel-invariance},
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:combined-condition-violation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{cond}} = 
        \mathcal{L}_{\text{dec}} + 
        \omega_{\text{inv}} \cdot \mathcal{L}_{\text{inv}},
\end{equation}
where $\omega_{\text{inv}}$ is a hyperparameter balancing the relative importance of the two loss components.
However, to apply the $\rho$-gate appropriately, $\mathcal{L}_{\text{cond}}$ is weighted for each sample using the soft sublevel weight
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:rho-gate}
    w_{\rho} = \operatorname{sigmoid} \left(
        s \cdot \frac{\rho - V(x_k)}{\max (\rho, \epsilon_{\text{rel}})}
    \right),
\end{equation}
where $s > 0$ denotes the gate sharpness. This smooth formulation ensures that the weights 
inside the $\rho$-sublevel are $\approx 1$, whereas the weights for states far outside of $\rho$ approach $0$. 
The weighted mean of the combined condition loss $\mathcal{L}_{\text{cond}}$ and $\rho$-gating weight $w_{\rho}$ 
results in the final loss formulation:
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:rho-gated-condition-loss}
    \mathcal{L}_{\rho\text{-gated}}
    = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^{N} w_{\rho}^{(i)} \cdot \mathcal{L}^{(i)}_{\text{cond}}}{\sum_{i=1}^{N} w_{\rho}^{(i)}}
\end{equation}


\paragraph{ROA-Surrogate Loss} tries to enlarge the region of attraction by creating samples within the 
boundary layer of the training space, defined as $\mathcal{B}_{\text{boundary}} = \mathcal{B} \setminus 0.6 \mathcal{B}$.
For a batch of $M$ samples $x_{\text{ROA}}^{(i)} \in \mathcal{B}_{\text{boundary}}$ are generated and evaluated in the following formulation:
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:roa-loss}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{ROA}} = \frac{1}{M} \sum_{i=1}^{M} \left[
        \frac{V(x_{\text{ROA}}^{(i)})}{\rho} - 1
    \right]_+.
\end{equation}


\paragraph{LIRPA-Condition Loss} smoothens the Lyapunov function by including formal violations of the Lyapunov decrease condition 
as defined in \cref{eq:lyapunov-decrease}, which are found using the LIRPA framework \cite{lirpabook}. 
In order to compute these violations efficiently, the LIRPA framework is provided with a filtered subset of regions. 
For the filter, the center $x_{\text{center}}$, lower bounds $x_{\text{lower}}$ and upper bounds $x_{\text{upper}}$ 
are used to evaluate whether the regions lie within the $\rho$-sublevel set or intersect its boundary. 
If at least one of the three characteristic points satisfies $V(x) \leq \rho$, the region is considered for evaluation. 
The model used for the LIRPA evaluation a signed Lyapunov decrease loss $-e_V(x_k, x_{k+1})$ as defined in \cref{eq:lyapunov-decrease}. 
This is done such that LIRPA can provide a lower bound $s_{\text{lower}}$ for the Lyapunov decrease condition, 
which is then used to define the LIRPA-condition loss.
After formally evaluating the signed Lyapunov decrease lower bound $s_{\text{lower}}$ for all regions, the LIRPA-condition loss is defined as:
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:relative-lirpa-condition}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{LIRPA}} = \frac{s_{\text{lower}}}{V(x_{\text{center}}) + \epsilon_{\text{rel}}}.
\end{equation}
