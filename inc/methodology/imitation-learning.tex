\section{Neural Policy Approximation and Imitation Learning}
\label{sec:imitation-learning}
This section specifies the data generation process, 
the neural policy architecture, and the training objective used to approximate the 
model predictive control (MPC) expert. Specifically, the MPC controller 
introduced in \cref{subsec:mpc-problem} serves as the expert policy 
generating the required state-input pairs. The neural policy is subsequently trained to 
reproduce the expert control actions over the considered state domain.


\subsection{Data Generation and MPC Expert}
\label{subsec:mpc-datagen}
The MPC controller introduced in \cref{subsec:mpc-problem} is used as the
expert policy and is denoted by $\pi_0$. For a given state $x$, it returns the
first input of the optimal MPC solution,
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:mpc-expert-policy}
    \pi_0(x) = u_0^*(x).
\end{equation}

The MPC expert is evaluated along closed-loop trajectories initialized from
states sampled within the admissible state space $\mathcal{X}$. At each time
step $k$, the current state $x_k$ and the first control action $u_0^*(x_k)$
returned by the MPC solver are stored as one training pair. If the MPC problem
becomes infeasible at any time during one trajectory, all training pairs
associated with that trajectory are discarded.

To reduce the overrepresentations of regions with high sampling rates, near-duplicate
state-action pairs are removed during preprocessing. The concatenated
state-action vectors are normalized and mapped onto an adaptive voxel grid.
For each occupied voxel, at most one representative pair is retained.



\subsection{Policy Network Architecture and Imitation Objective}
\label{subsec:imitation-architecture}
The policy architecture follows the structure proposed in \cite{yangLyapunovstableNeuralControl2024}, 
as it enforces true control inputs at the equilibrium $\pi_\theta(x^*) = u^*$ and 
satisfies the control input constraints $u_{\min} \leq \pi_\theta(x) \leq u_{\max}$ 
by construction. The neural policy $\pi_\theta$ is visualized in \cref{fig:imitation-policy} and forumlated as 
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:policy-arch}
    \pi_\theta(x) = \clamp \left( 
            f_\theta(x) - f_\theta(x^*) + u^*, 
            u_{\min},
            u_{\max}
    \right).
\end{equation}
where $\clamp(\cdot,u_{\min},u_{\max})$ is applied element-wise.

\paragraph{Imitation Objective} consists of a state-weighted behavioral cloning loss 
and a dynamics-aware penalty. 
The loss terms are normalized using the diagonal scaling matrices
\begin{equation}
    S_x = \diag(x_{\mathrm{scale}})
    \quad \text{and} \quad
    S_u = \diag(u_{\mathrm{scale}}), 
\end{equation}
where all scaling factors are strictly positive. In addition,
the element-wise rectified linear unit (ReLU) is defined as
\begin{equation}
    [z]_+ = \max(z,0).
\end{equation}

The normalized distance of the equilibrium is evaluated using the scaled $L_1$-norm: 
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:state-distance}
    d(x) = \frac{1}{n_x} \left\| S_x^{-1}(x - x^*) \right\|_1.
\end{equation}

To assign appropriate priority during behavioral cloning, a state-dependent weight is defined as
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:state-weight}
    w_x(x) = w_{\min} + (1 - w_{\min}) \left( \frac{1}{1 + d(x)} \right)^\alpha,
\end{equation}
where $w_{\min} \in (0, 1]$ is the baseline weight for states far from the
equilibrium and $\alpha \geq 0$ controls the decay rate.

\paragraph{State-Weighted Behavioral Cloning Loss} penalizes the normalized Euclidean distance 
between the neural network policy and the expert targets utilizing the state-dependent weighting. 
For a batch $\mathcal{X}_{\pi}$ containing $M$ samples, the behavioral cloning loss is
defined as
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:state-weighted-loss}
    \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{BC}} = \frac{1}{M n_u} 
    \sum_{x \in \mathcal{X}_\pi} w_x(x) \left\| 
        S_u^{-1} \big( \pi_\theta(x) - \pi_0(x) \big) 
    \right\|_2^2.
\end{equation}


\paragraph{Dynamics-Aware Constraint Loss} applies one-step penalties for successor states 
that leave the admissible training set $\mathcal{B}_\pi \subseteq \mathcal{X}$. 
For each state $x \in \mathcal{X}_{\pi}$, the next state is evaluated under 
the neural policy as $x_{k+1} = f(x,\pi_\theta(x))$. The loss is defined as
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:dynamics-aware-loss}
    \mathcal{L}_f = \frac{1}{M n_x} \sum_{x \in \mathcal{X}_\pi} \left( 
        \left\| 
            \big[ x_{k+1} - \bar{x}_\pi \big]_+ 
        \right\|_1 + \left\| 
            \big[ \underline{x}_\pi - x_{k+1} \big]_+ 
        \right\|_1 
    \right).
\end{equation}


\paragraph{Total Imitation Loss} is the combination of the state-weighted behavioral cloning 
and dynamics-aware constraint losses into a single scalar objective. 
Scaling each term by its respective weight, $\omega_{\mathrm{BC}}$ and $\omega_f$, 
yields the overall policy loss:
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:combined-policy-loss}
    \mathcal{L}_\pi = 
        \omega_{\mathrm{BC}} \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{BC}} + 
        \omega_f \mathcal{L}_f.
\end{equation}
