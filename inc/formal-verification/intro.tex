When using neural networks as control policies in safety-critical domains, it is essential to provide 
formal guarantees of safety, robustness, and stability for all states within the region of interest. 
Verification methods for neural networks are generally categorized into two approaches: 
\emph{Incomplete verifiers} efficiently compute sound lower bounds using convex relaxations, but may yield inconclusive 
results if the over-approximation gap is too large.
In contrast, \emph{complete verifiers} provide a definite decision by reducing this over-approximation gap, 
ensuring that the prescribed condition is either formally proven or a concrete counterexample is found. 

Early complete verifiers typically used exact non-convex activation functions with specialized 
Satisfiability Modulo Theories (SMT)~\cite{katzReluplexEfficientSMT2017} or 
Mixed-Integer Linear Programming (MILP)~\cite{tjengEvaluatingRobustnessNeural2019} solvers.
Although these solvers guarantee completeness, their complexity restricts their scalability on larger networks, 
which has led to the development of branch-and-bound (BaB)~\cite{bunelUnifiedViewPiecewise2018} 
frameworks that utilize parallelized incomplete bounds.

To perform scalable formal verification, this work relies on the $\alpha,\beta$-CROWN 
framework~\cite{wang2021beta,xuFastCompleteEnabling2021}, which utilizes GPU parallelization and efficient bound propagation. 
This chapter covers the theoretical foundations of the verification methods used in $\alpha,\beta$-CROWN: 
\cref{sec:incom-verification} introduces incomplete verification based on linear bound propagation via CROWN
~\cite{zhangEfficientNeuralNetwork2018} and its optimized variant $\alpha$-CROWN~\cite{xuFastCompleteEnabling2021}. 
The subsequent \cref{sec:comp-verification} discusses complete verification using branch-and-bound in combination with 
$\beta$-CROWN~\cite{wang2021beta} to soundly verify properties subject to neuron split constraints.
